# Fallow: references for the `vie` (life) and `survivants` (survivors) modules

Date: 26 October 2026. Game design reference research.

## Method and reliability

I opened about twenty pages. Points taken from an opened page are cited as they are. Points marked (to check) come only from a search summary: the page was closed (403, 402) or empty when opened. I never give them as certain. The pages for Ōkami (PC Gamer), Valheim, Stardew Valley and Tarrey Town could not be opened.

## 1. Minecraft mods and modpacks

**MineColonies.** The tavern welcomes visitors "periodically". They are recruited with items. One tavern per colony, 4 residents, level 3 maximum. Upgrading the tavern brings "more visitors and better visitors". Sleeping improves happiness (sleep page, not opened, search summary). The good: a single, readable arrival point, a clear cap. To avoid: a happiness system to manage all the time, which becomes a management chore. To take: an arrival tied to a place, not to an invisible roll.

**Millénaire.** The NPCs build and upgrade their houses on their own. The player can rise in the ranks of a culture (page opened, very short). Reputation through trade, gifts and quests is described in a search summary (to check). The good: a village that lives without the player. To avoid: reputation that is lost if you steal from a chest, too punishing for a world shared among friends. To take: residents who have a visible activity.

**Minecraft Comes Alive.** System of hearts, gifts and marriage, with different thresholds depending on the source (50 or 100 hearts). I could not open a usable page (to check). To keep as a negative: marriage, children and repeated gifts create grind and go against the "no reproduction" rule.

**Villager Recruits.** Villagers are recruited with emeralds to form an army. The opened page gives neither upkeep cost nor cap. To avoid: recruiting with money, which turns the resident into a bought unit.

**Guard Villagers.** Six guards per village. A villager without a job is converted by holding a weapon. Following is only open with "Hero of the Village", and the guard's inventory is only open to players with high reputation. The good: following is earned, and access is conditional. To take: following reserved for the one who has earned trust.

**Easy Villagers and leash mods.** Easy Villagers picks up villagers like items and adds blocks that automate trading, iron farms and breeding. Its promise: "never be bothered by villagers again". This is the proof that the vanilla system is a chore. It is also the model of the exploit: trade cycling, block breeding, iron farm. Villager on a Leash lets you hold a villager on a leash (no trust rule). To avoid: anything that makes the resident manipulable like an object. To take: following must go through the lantern, not through a neutral tool.

**Sculk (vanilla).** The catalyst activates when an entity dies within 8 blocks (Java). Only the nearest catalyst reacts. XP becomes charge at 1 to 1, a block holds 1000 charges at most, and sensors are limited per area (page opened). The good: the spread is gradual and visible, with local caps. To avoid: sculk feeds on deaths, so it is farmed with a mob trap. Same problem for the life counter if it is fed by an event that can be repeated without limit.

**Receding corruption: Terraria, Thaumcraft, Ōkami.** Terraria: purification is expensive, and purified blocks convert back "unless quarantined" (page opened). The game warns that purification has a price in money and work. Thaumcraft: flux is cleaned by hand or with a scrubber (search summary, to check). Ōkami: you make a guardian tree bloom and the curse lifts (search summary, to check). Lesson: the pleasure is in the visible transformation, not in the quarantine work. Fallow already has the right choice: the conversion is permanent, nothing is lost again.

**Twilight Forest.** Each defeated boss makes the barrier of the next region disappear (search summary, to check). Barriers without a clue are judged too restrictive by some players. To take: an unlock that announces itself (a clue, a sound) rather than a silent wall.

## 2. Other games

**Death Stranding.** Players do not meet: they leave structures and messages. Structures degrade under the "timefall". "Likes" give statistics and reputation. Reconnecting an area opens its maps and blueprints (Wikipedia, opened). The good: friends' traces are a relief when found, and the network is earned by walking. To avoid: the hunt for likes as a goal. To take: a survivor is a "connection" that opens something real in the clearing.

**Frostpunk.** Scouts bring back survivors. They must be housed, otherwise "better not let them in". Two gauges, hope and discontent, can lead to the end of the game (Game Informer, GamingBolt, opened). The good: the arrival is a choice with a cost. To avoid: gauges that punish up to failure. Fallow must never lose what it has gained.

**Banished.** Residents are born, work and die. Keeping everyone healthy and happy makes the town grow. Building houses is not enough (Steam description, search summary). Growth by birth is excluded for Fallow. To keep in mind: a population that grows can quickly overflow.

**Valheim.** Comfort comes from furniture under a roof, and the "Rested" buff lasts a time tied to comfort. Identical decorations count only once (Mobalytics and Shockbyte guides, not opened, to check). To take: the principle "duplicates do not count", very useful against exploits.

**Don't Starve.** Night and darkness lower sanity: about -5 per minute at dusk, -50 in total darkness. Fire helps, and the stronger it is, the more it helps (page opened). The good: light has a felt effect, never a number to watch. To avoid: fear through hallucinations, too close to the horror register that Fallow wants to avoid. To take: survivors soothed near the lantern, with no sanity mechanic.

**Dwarf Fortress.** The number of migrants depends mostly on the wealth created by the fortress. The first two waves are guaranteed. Deaths scare the arrivals, and selling all your wealth stops immigration (pages 23a and 40d, search summary, to check). Waves rarely exceed 24. To take: a guaranteed arrival at the start, then tied to prosperity, with caps. To avoid: the hidden mechanic that only experts understand.

**Kingdom Two Crowns.** You drop a coin, the vagrant picks it up and joins the camp. A piece of equipment sets their trade. Cutting the trees around a camp can make it disappear (GeekCulture opened, plus a search summary for the camps). The good: a single, readable gesture, no menu. To avoid: the loss of tools if attackers get through, and the camp that disappears by accident. To take: a simple gesture (lighting the lantern) as the only way in.

**RimWorld.** Colonists arrive through events: a chased refugee, a crashed pod, a wanderer, a recruited prisoner. The pace depends on the storyteller: beyond a certain number of colonists, some storytellers harden the events (search summary, to check). The good: each arrival has a context and a minimal story. To avoid: recruiting prisoners, a heavy management mechanic. To take: the arrival as a small scene ("someone is stuck in this ruin").

**Dragon Quest Builders.** The town's base level rises with the quality and quantity of buildings, and attracts more residents. It never goes down, even if monsters attack (DQB, opened). Each villager has a room preference: size, elegance, mood (Room preference, opened). The good: the level that never goes back down, and individual preferences. To avoid: a room limit felt as a wall. To take: a survivor with a precise taste.

**The Long Dark.** Almost no human presence in sandbox mode. The creator says that "walking slowly in the woods is part of survival" and that story mode has characters who "interfere with the solitude" (Kotaku, opened). To take: a survivor must remain a rare event, otherwise the solitude of the world is broken.

**Outward.** No guidance, three factions, quests with outcomes that change the world (Wikipedia, opened). Mixed reception (Metacritic 62 to 67). The "no hand-holding" appeals to a niche. To take with measure: the clue must exist, but be discreet.

**Spiritfarer.** Each spirit has its story, its tastes (food, housing, neighbors), its requests. It leaves for good when it is ready (WayTooManyGames, opened). The good: one person, one story. To avoid: the final loss, too sad for Fallow. To take: one story per survivor, revealed through care and preferences.

**Ōkami, Ori.** Ōkami: you paint to make a cursed area bloom, the reward is visual (search summary, to check). Ori: the world regains its balance in stages tied to the elements, and the art and music are the most praised points (Wikipedia Ori, opened). To take: healing must be seen and heard.

**Breath of the Wild, Tarrey Town.** Residents with names ending in "-son" are recruited, scattered across the map, and the houses appear on the map at each stage (search summary, to check). To take: each recruit comes from a precise place, with a clue.

**Animal Crossing.** The island rating combines Development and Scenery, with caps (190 tree points). A grid with 45 of 64 squares occupied is "cluttered", and more than 15 items on the ground makes the island "messy". Isabelle gives vague advice (Nintendo Life, opened). To take: the clutter penalty and the vague advice.

**Stardew Valley, Community Center.** Each bundle gives a reward, each room a bigger reward, and the full restoration a ceremony (search summary, to check). To take: milestones with offerings, with a ceremony.

## 3. What works everywhere

1. A visible milestone is better than a gauge to watch (Animal Crossing, Stardew).
2. Arrivals have a place, a context and a housing cost (Frostpunk, RimWorld, Tarrey Town).
3. What never goes down is more pleasant (Dragon Quest Builders). But this requires clear caps.
4. Duplicates do not count (Valheim, Animal Crossing).
5. Any repeatable event flow is farmable (sculk, Easy Villagers).

## Recommendations for the vie and survivants modules

1. **Feed life by variety, not by volume.** Each source counts once per species or per plant. A first harvest of wheat, carrot, berry counts. The hundred and first does not. Same for the zoo species. This is the Valheim "duplicate does not count" principle and the Animal Crossing variety.

2. **Daily cap per source.** Each category (harvests, births, nights slept, zoo) has a cap per game day and per clearing. A giant farm does not fill the gauge faster than a small varied garden.

3. **Against AFK.** Count only actions done by a player who is present in the clearing and recently active (movement, interaction). Exclude hoppers, dispensers, fake players, pistons and redstone. Births count only if the animal was born from food given by a player, and only while the number of animals in the clearing is under a threshold. A night slept counts once per player and per night, on a bed of the clearing. No counting of mob deaths, to avoid the sculk trap.

4. **Spaced thresholds, clear cap.** Rising tiers (for example 20, 50, 100, 180, 300 points) to go from +8 to +8. A radius cap specific to each clearing source (fire, lantern, dissipateur (dissipator)), stated clearly in the notebook. The counter never goes down, so there is no crisis management.

5. **Show growth as an event.** When a tier is crossed, the mist recedes from the center over 30 to 60 seconds. The grass turns green in a ring, fireflies or petals rise, a note of the clearing's music opens, a clear sound is heard. Blocks are converted in small batches each tick, so as not to crash the server. The next morning, a small poetic message in French is enough.

6. **No numeric gauge on screen.** The lantern shows the state: a higher or clearer flame when life rises, like Isabelle's vague advice. A single written clue, such as "La clairière respire plus largement" (The clearing breathes more widely), when a tier is near.

7. **Finding a survivor without a marker.** A single discreet clue per survivor: a thin smoke above a ruin, a window light, a song, a cloth tied to a branch. Deterministic placement tied to the seed and the ruins, one survivor for N ruins, never random at each visit. No compass. A notebook records what has been seen. This keeps the rarity of The Long Dark and the freedom of Outward, with the minimal clue of Tarrey Town.

8. **Recognizing them.** Silhouette and color specific to each survivor, sitting near a small ember, who raises their head when a player approaches with a lit lantern. Without a lantern, they stay still. The entry gesture is unique: holding out the lantern, like the coin in Kingdom Two Crowns.

9. **One survivor, one story.** Ten to twelve hand-written characters in French, each with a former trade, an object, a taste (a plant, a piece of music, a corner of the clearing), in the manner of Spiritfarer and Dragon Quest Builders. Three story moments, unlocked by time spent in the clearing and a simple offering, not by grind. The text goes through signs, placed objects, short sentences. No permanent departure.

10. **What they bring.** A trade, a skill and a trade offer per survivor, never two of the same family in one clearing. Trades come from a fixed, filtered table, with no emerald cycle and no reroll (the lesson of Easy Villagers). Gains are comfort and exploration (map, rare seeds, repair, cooking) more than raw power. A trade can unlock the next part of their story.

11. **Fear and safety.** Outside the clearing, a survivor crouches, refuses to work and goes quiet. They never die and never get lost: if abandoned, they return to their ember. No discontent gauge like Frostpunk. Following with the lantern: only one leader at a time per survivor, and at most two followers per player.

12. **Anti-clutter.** The capacity of a clearing is tied to its radius (for example one survivor per +8 tier, cap of 4 to 6). A world population cap. Simple, decorative housing (bench, blanket, ember) is enough, no beds to manage. The clearing's count ignores duplicate decor, like the Animal Crossing clutter penalty.

13. **The attraction of a new clearing.** Only when a clearing passes a high tier, with a long rearm delay (several real days), only once per tier, and never if the world cap is reached. The arrival is announced by smoke on the horizon, not by a teleporter. This is the Dwarf Fortress prosperity logic, with the RimWorld population brake.

14. **Multiplayer fairness.** Life belongs to the clearing, not to a player. All present players contribute to it, with a contribution cap per player and per day, so that a grinder does not get ahead of the others. The first to find a survivor does not "own" them: following passes from hand to hand, and trades are identical for everyone. Absent players do not count. Traces can be left for friends (sign, tied cloth), in the spirit of Death Stranding.

15. **Technical.** Life counter in a `SavedData` per clearing, updated by events, not by chunk sweeps. A log of event sources to debug exploits. Block conversion with a per-tick budget.
