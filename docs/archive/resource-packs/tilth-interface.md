# The `tilth-interface` resource pack

> **Archived on 2026-11-13.** The maintainer removed the three house resource packs (`tilth-interface`, `tilth-ambiance`, `tilth-textures`) from the pack ([0024](../../decisions/0024-removing-the-house-resource-packs.md)). This page records what they contained and how the mods read them, should any part come back.

Tilth's interface: vanilla screens, the screens of the mods, the HUD and the Questlog journal, in one style. It replaced Dark Coffee GUI and Modded Coffee GUI on 2026-10-26 (all rights reserved, and their author does not answer).

## Sources

The maintainer asked to start from an existing pack that may be modified, then adjust it to the pack's mods. After comparing about thirty interface packs for 1.21.1, the chosen mix is:

| Pack | Author | Licence | What it brings |
|---|---|---|---|
| GUI Revision 2.2.0 | Vixel | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | Vanilla screens and HUD |
| GUI Revision Compats 43.0 | Fyoncle | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | Screens of Supplementaries, Amendments, Quark, Cold Sweat, Curios, EMI, JEI, Exposure, Farm & Charm, Immersive Enchanting, Overgeared, Chipped, Naturalist, Entity Texture Features |
| Questlog | Infernal Studios | Apache-2.0 | The journal's layout (redrawn) |
| Minecraft 1.21.1 client | Mojang | Minecraft EULA | The crafter's screen and the widgets GUI Revision lacks |

Rejected: Overgrown Flowery GUI (its licence forbids publishing a modified version), STONEBORN and Whimscape (all rights reserved), CozyUI+ (GPL-3, but its high-definition look feels like an app rather than Minecraft).

Licence of the result: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, like GUI Revision. Tilth must therefore not be used commercially (no paid access, no revenue sharing on the pack, monetisation off on its Modrinth page). The recoloured textures of other mods stay under their own licences, listed in `pack/resourcepacks/tilth-interface/CREDITS.md`; only mods whose licence allows a modified copy to be shared are recoloured, so Adorable Hamster Pets, Critters and Companions and Mowzie's Mobs (all rights reserved; No Man's Land was in the list until it left the pack) keep their own screens.

## Build

`scripts/interface/build.py` (Python 3 + Pillow 12.1 or newer, authoring tool, not run by CI) downloads the pinned releases and the game's client (checked by SHA-1), keeps only the textures the game or a mod of the pack reads (compat files made for other versions of a mod, OptiFine's and the files left as they were are dropped, and a texture keeps the nine-slice metadata of the one it replaces), and writes the pack and its icon. It stops if a texture named in one of its coordinate tables is no longer found. Run `npm run check-fr` once before, so that the mod jars are in `.cache/jars`. Then `packwiz refresh` in `pack/`.

What the script changes:

- **Colours.** GUI Revision draws everything with a handful of greys (outline, darks, slots, panel, light), and its relief comes from them. Each grey maps to Tilth's mist (`palette.py`): moss darks and outline `#1A1F1C`, sage slots `#828C7A`, mist panels `#CBCAB6`, bone light `#E8E5D0`. Each kind of screen then takes a family of colours on its top panel, built from the same greys at the same brightness so that every bevel stays as GUI Revision drew it: wood for the crafting table, the loom, the cartography table and the traders (whose whole screen takes it), forge (warm ash) for the three furnaces, moss for the anvil, the grindstone, the stonecutter, the smithing table, the dispenser and the crafter, dusk (soft purple) for the enchanting table, the brewing stand and the beacon, the End's purple for the shulker box. The player's inventory below stays mist on every screen, as do the inventory, the chests, the hoppers, the mounts and the mods' screens. No screen keeps GUI Revision's blue-grey. Tilth's details (`details.py`) are drawn in the mist, then take the family of the panel they lie on by brightness.
- **Screens in other colours.** GUI Revision paints a few vanilla screens in wood or purple (crafting table, loom, cartography table, enchanting table, shulker box); they are first brought back to its greys, so that they take their family. The same goes for Chipped's workbench and Quark's backpack.
- **Leaves.** One small leaf, tone on tone, repeated on a single staggered grid anchored to the texture's corner over every large stretch of panel, in the panel's family: the pattern runs on unbroken and passes under the slots, the frames and the corner ornaments drawn over it.
- **Slots.** Every slot is redrawn as Tilth's: a sage well with rounded corners, its outline darker at the top and left and fading towards the ends, lighter at the bottom and right, without GUI Revision's dark top run (its cast shadow) or its light lip. Where four slots meet, a small cross of panel stays between them. Placeholders drawn in a slot (banner, dye, pattern) are kept. The same slot is used in mod screens drawn in vanilla's greys, the hotbar, JEI and EMI, the bundle's tooltip and the crafter's disabled slot (filled up to the panel).
- **Wells.** Where the player and the mounts stand (the inventory, the creative inventory, the mount's screen, Quark's backpack, Naturalist's elephant), a deeper well laid with small tiles in two tones and their joints.
- **Crafting frames.** The crafting table's frame (four pixels each side and a shadow under it) is laid around every other 2x2 or 3x3 grid that has six pixels of bare panel around it; slim (outline and inner row, no shadow) where the name's plate comes near.
- **Arrows.** Tilth's own arrow, engraved in ink with a pixel of light under it: a two-pixel shaft, a barbed head of two slim diagonals and a notch of fletching at the tail. It replaces GUI Revision's furnace arrow and its small triangles (crafting table, anvil, smithing table, grindstone, trader, cartography table), comes back to the inventory (shorter), points down on the brewing stand, and replaces vanilla's wide arrow copied by mods, Farm & Charm's and Kaleidoscope Cookery's arrows, and JEI's and EMI's. The trader's offers take a small one. Progress sprites (furnaces, brewing stand, JEI, EMI) are the same arrow in the Questlog's lantern light. The plus (anvil, cartography table, JEI, EMI, Exposure) is slim, with two-pixel bars, engraved the same way.
- **Details.** Every large panel keeps GUI Revision's frame and relief, and takes a corner ornament from one of four families of GUI Revision's own (its crafting table's, its enchanting table's runes, its chest's rule, its ender chest's key), in the colours of its family, in all four corners of its main stretch of panel, or the small round ornament in all four where a slot, a widget or the name's plate takes a corner. The crafting and enchanting tables keep their own. The ornaments lie on the leaves, with a pixel of bare panel around.
- **HUD.** The hotbar is redrawn as a mist bar with rounded ends and a soft shadow, Tilth's slots in it; the selection, rounded like a slot, and the offhand slot match it, the selection in the Questlog's lantern light. Without the XP bar, the `tilth` mod (0.4.1, `xp` module) lowers hearts, armor, hunger, air and thirst by 5 pixels, and Cold Sweat's body icon follows through its client config ([mod-tilth](../../plan/mod-tilth.md)).
- **Widgets.** `scripts/interface/widgets.py` draws the buttons, sliders, checkboxes, tabs, text fields, scrollers, small square buttons and the recipe book's toggles anew. Raised parts are sage plaques with a moss outline, rounded corners, a bone light on top, a shadow below and an engraved inner frame; recessed parts (the slider's track, checkboxes, text fields) are moss wells shaded like the slots. A rivet is engraved at each end of the buttons. A button the pointer is on fills with the lantern's warm light, a well (slider, text field, checkbox) lights its outline; a disabled button is darker and flat. The checkboxes are nine-sliced so that vanilla's 17-pixel box keeps its tick whole.
- **Sprites.** Toasts, the advancements' boxes and frames, the recipe book, the server and world lists and the containers' sprites take Tilth's tones by brightness; what GUI Revision leaves purple, wood or blue (the enchanting table's choices, the loom's patterns, the beacon's buttons) is brought to them too, and highlights glow in lantern light. Colours that carry a meaning (red crosses, green ticks, pings, boss bars) stay. What is selected (the creative and advancement tabs, the beacon's effect, the stonecutter's and loom's choice) lights up in the lantern's light, where GUI Revision drew it darker. The advancements' goal and challenge frames keep the game's round and notched outlines, in the colours of the task's square one. The heart containers flashing when hurt have a bone outline, a disabled enchantment and the anvil's unusable name field are a step darker, the crafter's powered arrow glows, and the book's page arrows, the menus' icons (language, accessibility, news, link), the attack indicator and the empty slots' placeholders (armour, smithing) are taken from the game in Tilth's tones; the nether's and husbandry's advancement backgrounds are brought halfway to the moss. Vanilla textures GUI Revision does not cover are taken from the game's jar and dressed alike: the crafter's screen (its grid framed like the crafting table's), text fields, tabs, checkboxes, scrollers, the bundle's and the crafter's sprites, the popup.
- **Menus.** The main menu, the options and the other menus share the same tones: the shading laid over their background and the darker band behind their lists are a plain moss veil, and the separators under their header and above their footer are moss and bone instead of black and white, in game too; the statistics' sprites and the demo's panel are dressed like the rest. The panorama behind the menus opened from the title screen (the title screen itself has a painting, below), in place of vanilla's trial chamber, shows the idea of the pack rather than a landscape: `scripts/interface/panorama.py` paints a world in mist and a clearing that a light has opened in it, the mist being a volume the clearing cuts like a cylinder, so the dome overhead, the wall at its edge and the fading of what lies beyond come from one rule. Three variants: `vigil` (the default: dusk in a small clearing around a vigil fire, fireflies, a deer at the edge of the mist), `threshold` (by day at the edge of a lantern's clearing, a sown field behind, standing stones and a monolith fading into the mist ahead) and `lifted` (from above a clearing a dissipateur keeps, a sea of mist to the horizon holed by other clearings, far ranges rising out of it). `DEFAULT` picks the one built.
- **Title and loading screens.** FancyMenu and Drippy Loading Screen (client only) carry two layouts written by `scripts/interface/menus.py` into `pack/config/fancymenu/`. The title screen's background is Caspar David Friedrich's *Riesengebirge Landscape with Rising Fog* (c. 1820, Neue Pinakothek, Munich; public domain; the Web Gallery of Art's scan on Wikimedia Commons, cut to 16:9), chosen by the maintainer over a painted scene of ours: a GLSL background (`scripts/interface/title/mist.glsl`, Shadertoy style) never moves or warps the painting, and lets mist of its own colour flow sideways through its valleys, over a thick base, kept off the sky and the dark slopes in front by a mask drawn from the painting (`title/mist-mask.png`: its pale, grey, finely detailed mist, the sky cut, blurred). `STRENGTH` (0.75) sets how thick the mist gets, `SPEED` (5) how fast it flows: a near ribbon crosses the screen in about thirteen seconds. Over it, no logo for now (the pack's logo is to be redesigned; Minecraft's stays hidden), no splash text, over a moss veil that darkens the bottom and the sides; the vanilla buttons and the required texts stay. The other menus keep the panorama. The loading screen has a moss ground and a bar in lantern light instead of Mojang's red screen and logo. The former logo (worn stone uncials with moss and a lantern, `logo.py`) was removed on 2026-11-13 at the team's request. FancyMenu runs in modpack mode: players never see its editor. NeoForge's early loading window, before the mods load, keeps its own look.
- **Names.** Every container screen's name stands centred over its top edge on GUI Revision's plate, overhanging the panel by three pixels, in its engraved capitals: five pixels tall, a step darker than the plate, a pixel of light under each stroke. GUI Revision draws that plate on five screens with their English names baked in; those are removed, and `lettering.py` completes its alphabet in the same hand (accents, Œ, digits, signs). The `titres` module of the `tilth` mod draws the plate and the name in game ([mod-tilth](mod-tilth.md)), so the name follows the game's language, a renamed chest and the trader's level, and mod screens can take it; everything it draws comes from this pack: `assets/tilth/titres.json` (each screen's top edge and family, the families' ink and light, the letters' table, the mods whose screens take a plate), a nine-slice plate per family and the letters' atlas. The inventory label is blank in every screen (`lang/`). The beacon keeps its two labels on its dark well. Mod screens that write their titles themselves keep them, in moss green: the pack's text shader (`shaders/core/rendertype_text.fsh`, the game's own with one test) recolours the game's label grey (`0x404040`).
- **Other mods.** Screens of mods without a compat that use vanilla's greys and whose licence allows it (Envelope, Kaleidoscope Cookery, Alex's Mobs, Companions!, TreeChop) are recoloured in the same tones; a mod's own plain grey surfaces follow the same ramp. The mod's own drawings (Chipped's tool rack, the 3D blocks of Kaleidoscope's recipes, Companions!' puppet…) keep their colours: only the menu itself is reworked. Create and its addons keep their own style, as do the config libraries and the books (Patchouli, Modonomicon, Wildex).
- **Questlog.** `scripts/interface/questlog.py` draws the journal in the same light style: mist cover with sage trims and curled corners, linen pages, sage tiles, lantern light for what is selected, the search bar written « Rechercher ».

## Order

Default Options enables it above the Fresh Animations packs and below Supernova and `tilth-ambiance`.

## Limits and in-game tests

1. Names: the tests E1 to E7 of [mod-tilth](../../plan/mod-tilth.md#titles) (every vanilla container, a renamed chest, the trader at each level, the recipe book open, GUI scales 2 to 4, French and English, a few mod screens); check also that the titles mod screens write themselves are moss green and that signs and other texts keep their colours. The shader only touches `0x404040`; a mod drawing text with its own shader keeps its grey.
2. Check the hotbar, hearts and selected slot at GUI scales 2, 3 and 4, and the Questlog at its default and custom panel sizes (the nine-slice edges are stretched).
3. Check the screens of Overgeared, Cold Sweat and Supplementaries (the traders' offer bar), whose compats target the latest versions of those mods; compat files for other versions (old JEI, Immersive Enchanting, Curios and Supplementaries textures) are not shipped, so JEI keeps its own look (its lists are hidden).
4. Companions! draws English words into its card game textures (Win, Lost, Card, Stop); they come from the mod itself.
5. Thirst Was Reclaimed and Cold Sweat draw their own HUD icons; to be matched later.
6. The FancyMenu and Drippy layouts are written by hand, never opened in their editor: check that the title screen shows no logo (neither Minecraft's nor ours) and no splash text, over the painting the right way up, its mist flowing through the valleys and not over the sky (on Windows and macOS; if the shader fails to compile, FancyMenu shows nothing behind the buttons), and that the loading screen after a resource reload (F3+T) is moss green with a lantern-coloured bar only. If a layout is ignored, open it once in FancyMenu's editor (set `modpack_mode` to false in `config/fancymenu/options.txt`) and save it.
7. Widgets: the buttons' hover filled with the lantern's light and readable, a ticked checkbox in the options (17-pixel box), the sliders' knob, the tabs of the world creation screen.
8. Details: the game's white hover square over the rounded slots, the crafter's disabled slot and the bundle's tooltip on the rounded wells, and JEI's brewing recipe, whose lit arrow is assumed drawn at (42, 2) over the one laid on its background.

## History

Before this pack, two drawn-from-scratch iterations (2026-10-19, then two on 2026-10-26: leather and parchment, then moss slate) were found not clean enough; the maintainer then asked to start from a free-licensed pack, and picked the GUI Revision + Hollow GUI mix. A first review then asked for a regular background pattern instead of scattered details, rounder slots, and every screen in harmony: hence the leaf pattern, the rounded slots and the greyed screens. A second review (same day) asked for the polish of Dark Coffee GUI without its pixels: no names drawn in textures, slimmer arrows, no light line over the slots, a tighter pattern at the same spacing everywhere, cast shadows, rounded slots, a textured well behind the player, a hotbar with rounded ends, and mods' own drawings left untouched. A third review asked for no dark line over the slots, rounded squares instead of round slots, and a finer well behind the player than the grid: the slots now take Dark Coffee's construction (outline, inner shadow, corner profile) redrawn in Tilth's colours, and the well its fine grain. A fourth review asked that nothing be cut or nearly overlap, and that the bars come down into the space of the XP bar. A fifth review asked for the crafting table's corners and curls on every screen, one leaf pattern that does not move with the slots, and a full grain behind the player without the shadow at its top. A sixth review asked for the mods' arrows, the stray light marks around the player, straight crosses between slots, and the crafting table's frame on the other crafting grids. A last full audit before release (vanilla screens, sprites, every mod screen, code and packaging) then fixed the sprites left in GUI Revision's greys and colours, the brewing stand's arrow, the enchanting table's frame, the hopper's open edge, leftovers of old arrows and wells in mod screens, the textures no version reads, the licences of recoloured mod textures, and added the crafter's screen and a pack icon. The menus were then brought to the same tones (shading, separators, a misty forest panorama), the widgets were redrawn (the hover state was darker than the rest state, and the lists' arrows turned green), and FancyMenu and Drippy were added for a Tilth title and loading screen. A review against every texture of Dark Coffee GUI (studied for its ideas, none of its pixels used) then brought the game's titles back (removing them was pointless, since the game translates them), in moss green; leaves and seals under the arrows and pluses, grooves around slots used together, a fire of its own for each furnace, the inventory's lost arrow, selected states in lantern light, the advancements' frame shapes and the sprites still in the game's colours; and gave the menus a leaf veil, riveted buttons filled with lantern light on hover and a veil on the title screen. A last review found the titles at the game's positions fell on the frames and ornaments, and the plus and the arrows off their medallions' centre: the titles moved onto a plaque drawn by the `tilth` mod, the medallions became flat ovals and seals centred on their mark to the pixel, and the anvil's and cartography table's spike became a true plus. The next review found the plaque and the plain medallions too simple, the curls one ornament too many once the titles were back, the corner ornaments missing on some screens and close to the beacon's buttons, and the furnaces' fires unfinished: the titles came back into the panels on plates (or bands) drawn by the pack, the `titres` module was removed, the arrows and pluses took lozenges after Dark Coffee GUI's idea, the corners took four families from GUI Revision with the all-or-small rule, each furnace a finished fire, the menus' veil lost its leaves, and the logo its new stone, moss, vines and lantern.

The review of 2026-10-26 evening found the result too far from GUI Revision: flat, badly framed, the names at the game's places. Everything was redone from GUI Revision's own drawing: its panels, frames and slots kept as they are, a family of colours per kind of screen instead of one palette, its furnace arrow everywhere instead of the slim one, no leaf pattern, wells, lozenges or grooves, and the names centred over the top edge on its plate and in its lettering, drawn by the `titres` module back in `tilth` 0.4.4.

The next morning (2026-10-27), on that base, the maintainer asked for Tilth's own details back: the leaf pattern as one continuous background passing under the slots, a slimmer arrow and plus in Tilth's style, the tiling behind the player, Tilth's rounded slots in place of GUI Revision's with their cast shadow, the crafting frames where they fit, and no screen left in the default grey. They came back in `details.py`, drawn in the mist and taken to each screen's family; the stone family became moss.
