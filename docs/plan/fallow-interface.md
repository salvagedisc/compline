# The `fallow-interface` resource pack

Fallow's interface: vanilla screens, the screens of the mods, the HUD and the Questlog journal, in one style. It replaced Dark Coffee GUI and Modded Coffee GUI on 2026-10-26 (all rights reserved, and their author does not answer).

## Sources

The maintainer asked to start from an existing pack that may be modified, then adjust it to the pack's mods. After comparing about thirty interface packs for 1.21.1, the chosen mix is:

| Pack | Author | Licence | What it brings |
|---|---|---|---|
| GUI Revision 2.2.0 | Vixel | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | Vanilla screens and HUD |
| GUI Revision Compats 43.0 | Fyoncle | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | Screens of Supplementaries, Amendments, Quark, Cold Sweat, Curios, EMI, JEI, Exposure, Farm & Charm, Immersive Enchanting, Overgeared, Chipped, Naturalist, Entity Texture Features |
| Hollow GUI 2 | Melee | CC BY 4.0 | The two curls at the top of the panels |
| Questlog | Infernal Studios | Apache-2.0 | The journal's layout (redrawn) |
| Minecraft 1.21.1 client | Mojang | Minecraft EULA | The crafter's screen and the widgets GUI Revision lacks |

Rejected: Overgrown Flowery GUI (its licence forbids publishing a modified version), STONEBORN and Whimscape (all rights reserved), CozyUI+ (GPL-3, but its high-definition look feels like an app rather than Minecraft).

Licence of the result: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, like GUI Revision. Fallow must therefore not be used commercially (no paid access, no revenue sharing on the pack, monetisation off on its Modrinth page). The recoloured textures of other mods stay under their own licences, listed in `pack/resourcepacks/fallow-interface/CREDITS.md`; only mods whose licence allows a modified copy to be shared are recoloured, so Adorable Hamster Pets, Critters and Companions, Mowzie's Mobs and No Man's Land (all rights reserved) keep their own screens.

## Build

`scripts/interface/build.py` (Python 3 + Pillow 12.1 or newer, authoring tool, not run by CI) downloads the pinned releases and the game's client (checked by SHA-1), keeps only the textures the game or a mod of the pack reads (compat files made for other versions of a mod, OptiFine's and the files left as they were are dropped, and a texture keeps the nine-slice metadata of the one it replaces), and writes the pack and its icon. It stops if a texture named in one of its coordinate tables is no longer found. Run `npm run check-fr` once before, so that the mod jars are in `.cache/jars`. Then `packwiz refresh` in `pack/`.

What the script changes:

- **Colours.** GUI Revision uses a handful of greys; each one maps to Fallow's palette: mist panels `#CBCAB6`, sage slots `#828C7A`, bone highlights `#E8E5D0`, moss darks and outline `#1A1F1C`.
- **Screens in other colours.** GUI Revision paints a few vanilla screens in wood or purple (crafting table, loom, cartography table, enchanting table, shulker box); they are first brought back to its greys, so that they take the same palette. The same goes for Chipped's workbench and Quark's backpack, whose leather pocket becomes a darker mist pocket with bone stitches.
- **Slots.** Every slot (vanilla, compats, recoloured mods, the hotbar) is redrawn over its whole cell as a recessed well with rounded corners: an outline a step darker than the well, darker at the top and left and fading towards the ends, a soft two-row shadow under the top edge, and a lighter edge along the bottom and right. There is no light line on top and no near-black edge, and no slot is round. A placeholder drawn inside a slot (the loom's banner and dye, Overgeared's blueprint) is kept. The wells where the player and the mounts stand (and the black ones of mod screens) are deeper, filled edge to edge with a fine two-tone grain, without the inner shadow. Each cut corner takes the colour beside it (a frame, a section border, transparency), so that only where four slots meet does a small cross of panel show, the same on every screen. Marks of the source's old slot art left within two pixels of a slot (light corners, a line along the player's well) are cleared. Every block of 2x2 to 3x3 slots with room around it (the inventory's crafting grid, the backpack's, the dispenser, the crafters of the game and of Quark) takes the crafting table's frame.
- **Arrows.** GUI Revision's wide arrows and small triangles, and vanilla's wide arrow copied by mods, become one engraved arrow with a two-pixel shaft and a solid head as tall as the arrow; the furnaces' progress arrow (and the copies of it in Overgeared, EMI…) is the same arrow in the Questlog's lantern light. Arrows mods draw in shapes of their own (Envelope, Exposure, Farm & Charm, Kaleidoscope Cookery's straight and bent ones) are found by shape, whatever their colour, and redrawn the same way, at 17x8 where the mod's progress sprite is that small; Exposure's plus, the anvil's and the cartography table's become engraved too. The brewing stand's arrow is the same one turned down, empty on the panel and lit in its progress sprite. The inventory's arrow, which GUI Revision leaves out, is drawn back at 13 pixels. After Dark Coffee GUI's idea (none of its pixels), every arrow lying on a panel crosses a sunken lozenge, its head reaching past it: a ground a step below the panel, its edge dark above and to the left and light below and to the right like the slots, small stepped triangles above and below the shaft pointing at it, centred on the arrow and smaller where the slots leave less room. Every plus (8x8, two-pixel bars) lies on a lozenge of the same make with a small L in each corner around it; the anvil's and the cartography table's plus, which GUI Revision draws as a small spike, are drawn again as a true plus; slots used together (the anvil's, the smithing table's, the grindstone's, the trader's, the loom's) are ringed with an engraved groove.
- **Details.** Every large panel, vanilla or mod, takes a corner ornament in the four corners of its main stretch of panel, from one of four families redrawn after GUI Revision's own top-left ornaments: its crafting table's (crafting screens, the anvil, the smithing table, the grindstone and most mods), its enchanting table's runes (enchanting, brewing, beacon, Curios, Immersive Enchanting), its chest's rule (chests, shulker boxes, mounts, loom, cartography, traders, storage mods) and its ender chest's key (furnaces, stonecutter, dispenser, hopper, cooking mods). Where one corner has no room for it (a slot, a text, a widget the game draws over the panel: the beacon's buttons and payment items, the anvil's cost, the recipe book's button), the panel takes the small round ornament in all four corners instead, so a screen never shows two or three; a corner a title's plate reaches is left to the plate. Every panel has the same frame: a moss outline, a bone light on top, a sage bevel on the sides (the enchanting table's double frame and its own corner marks are replaced). Every large panel is covered by one small leaf pattern on a single grid shared by all textures (every 10 pixels across, 8 down, staggered), one shade from the panel; it runs on between and under the slots, so it is the same on every screen whatever the layout. The furnaces each show their own fire, 14x14 where the game draws it lit, engraved on the panel and the same shape in the lit sprite the game uncovers from the bottom as the fuel burns: the furnace a flame with a second tongue over a hearth line, the blast furnace a tall white-hot flame over a grate, the smoker wisps of smoke over a low fire. Where a large well touches a slot (the player in the creative inventory, the horse), the well gives way by a pixel; cells a compat drew slightly differently inside a grid (Farm & Charm, the creative inventory's top row) are found from the grid's pitch and redrawn like the others.
- **HUD.** The hotbar is redrawn as a mist bar with rounded ends and a soft shadow, its slots like those of the screens; the selection and the offhand slot match it, the selection in the Questlog's lantern light. Without the XP bar, the `fallow` mod (0.4.1, `xp` module) lowers hearts, armor, hunger, air and thirst by 5 pixels, and Cold Sweat's body icon follows through its client config ([mod-fallow](mod-fallow.md)).
- **Widgets.** `scripts/interface/widgets.py` draws the buttons, sliders, checkboxes, tabs, text fields, scrollers, small square buttons and the recipe book's toggles anew. Raised parts are sage plaques with a moss outline, rounded corners, a bone light on top, a shadow below and an engraved inner frame; recessed parts (the slider's track, checkboxes, text fields) are moss wells shaded like the slots. A rivet is engraved at each end of the buttons. A button the pointer is on fills with the lantern's warm light, a well (slider, text field, checkbox) lights its outline; a disabled button is darker and flat. The checkboxes are nine-sliced so that vanilla's 17-pixel box keeps its tick whole.
- **Sprites.** Toasts, the advancements' boxes and frames, the recipe book, the server and world lists and the containers' sprites take Fallow's tones by brightness; what GUI Revision leaves purple, wood or blue (the enchanting table's choices, the loom's patterns, the beacon's buttons) is brought to them too, and highlights glow in lantern light. Colours that carry a meaning (red crosses, green ticks, pings, boss bars) stay. What is selected (the creative and advancement tabs, the beacon's effect, the stonecutter's and loom's choice) lights up in the lantern's light, where GUI Revision drew it darker. The advancements' goal and challenge frames keep the game's round and notched outlines, in the colours of the task's square one. The heart containers flashing when hurt have a bone outline, a disabled enchantment and the anvil's unusable name field are a step darker, the crafter's powered arrow glows, and the book's page arrows, the menus' icons (language, accessibility, news, link), the attack indicator and the empty slots' placeholders (armour, smithing) are taken from the game in Fallow's tones; the nether's and husbandry's advancement backgrounds are brought halfway to the moss. Vanilla textures GUI Revision does not cover are taken from the game's jar and dressed alike: the crafter's screen (its grid framed like the crafting table's), text fields, tabs, checkboxes, scrollers, the bundle's and the crafter's sprites, the popup.
- **Menus.** The main menu, the options and the other menus share the same tones: the shading laid over their background and the darker band behind their lists are a plain moss veil, and the separators under their header and above their footer are moss and bone instead of black and white, in game too; the statistics' sprites and the demo's panel are dressed like the rest. The title screen's panorama is a pine forest fading into the mist under a veiled sun, drawn by the script (four rows of pines, the nearest with two clearings), in place of vanilla's trial chamber.
- **Title and loading screens.** FancyMenu and Drippy Loading Screen (client only) carry two layouts written by `scripts/interface/menus.py` into `pack/config/fancymenu/`. The title screen shows the Fallow logo in place of Minecraft's and no splash text over a moss veil that darkens the panorama towards the bottom and the sides, and keeps the vanilla buttons, the panorama and the required texts. The loading screen has a moss ground, the logo and a bar in lantern light instead of Mojang's red screen. The logo, drawn by `scripts/interface/logo.py`, is the word in worn stone uncials, bone fading to mist (the Uncial Antiqua typeface by Astigmatic, SIL Open Font License), with moss on their tops, vines hanging from them, and a lit lantern on a chain inside the O, its light warming the stone. FancyMenu runs in modpack mode: players never see its editor. NeoForge's early loading window, before the mods load, keeps its own look.
- **Titles.** The game writes each screen's title where it always does, in the player's language (its own translations, and the mods' own files for theirs), in moss green: the pack's text shader (`shaders/core/rendertype_text.fsh`, the game's own with one test) recolours the game's label grey (`0x404040`). Under each title of a vanilla screen, `build.py` draws a plate in the texture, in the spirit of GUI Revision's named plates: bone with a sage edge, its ends cut at 45 degrees like the lozenges, a shade along its bottom, three pixels of bone either side of the longest French or English name that can stand there (`TITLES`, with the game's positions read from its 1.21.1 code: centred titles centred as the game rounds them, the trader's name with each level). Chests, shulker boxes, hoppers and mounts, which the player can name, take a band across the panel instead, so any name fits. No frame, groove or ornament runs under a plate; the crafting table's title sits on its grid's frame like a tab. The beacon's labels, on its dark well, keep the game's light grey and no plate. The inventory label is blank in every screen (`lang/`). The names GUI Revision drew into the textures are removed (the plates of the furnaces, the crafting and enchanting tables, the traders' "TRADES" banner and Supplementaries' "TRADE OFFER" box), since text drawn in a texture cannot follow the game's language. On mod screens, a grid frame the title would touch is drawn slim (outline and inner row).
- **Other mods.** Screens of mods without a compat that use vanilla's greys and whose licence allows it (Envelope, Kaleidoscope Cookery, Alex's Mobs, Companions!, TreeChop) are recoloured in the same tones; a mod's own plain grey surfaces follow the same ramp. The mod's own drawings (Chipped's tool rack, the 3D blocks of Kaleidoscope's recipes, Companions!' puppet…) keep their colours: only the menu itself is reworked. Create and its addons keep their own style, as do the config libraries and the books (Patchouli, Modonomicon, Wildex).
- **Questlog.** `scripts/interface/questlog.py` draws the journal in the same light style: mist cover with sage trims and curled corners, linen pages, sage tiles, lantern light for what is selected, the search bar written « Rechercher ».

## Order

Default Options enables it above the Fresh Animations packs and below Supernova and `fallow-ambiance`.

## Limits and in-game tests

1. Titles: check that each vanilla container (furnace, anvil, smithing table, chest, renamed chest, villager at each level, wandering trader…) shows its title on its plate or band, at GUI scales 2 to 4, with the recipe book open too, in French and in English; that mod screens' titles are moss green; and that signs and other texts keep their colours. The shader only touches `0x404040`; a mod drawing text with its own shader keeps its grey.
2. Check the hotbar, hearts and selected slot at GUI scales 2, 3 and 4, and the Questlog at its default and custom panel sizes (the nine-slice edges are stretched).
3. Check the screens of Overgeared, Cold Sweat and Supplementaries (the traders' offer bar), whose compats target the latest versions of those mods; compat files for other versions (old JEI, Immersive Enchanting, Curios and Supplementaries textures) are not shipped, so JEI keeps its own look (its lists are hidden).
4. Companions! draws English words into its card game textures (Win, Lost, Card, Stop); they come from the mod itself.
5. Thirst Was Reclaimed and Cold Sweat draw their own HUD icons; to be matched later.
6. The FancyMenu and Drippy layouts are written by hand, never opened in their editor: check that the title screen shows the Fallow logo without the Minecraft logo or splash text, and that the loading screen after a resource reload (F3+T) is moss green with the logo and a lantern-coloured bar. If a layout is ignored, open it once in FancyMenu's editor (set `modpack_mode` to false in `config/fancymenu/options.txt`) and save it.
7. Widgets: the buttons' hover filled with the lantern's light and readable, a ticked checkbox in the options (17-pixel box), the sliders' knob, the tabs of the world creation screen.

## History

Before this pack, two drawn-from-scratch iterations (2026-10-19, then two on 2026-10-26: leather and parchment, then moss slate) were found not clean enough; the maintainer then asked to start from a free-licensed pack, and picked the GUI Revision + Hollow GUI mix. A first review then asked for a regular background pattern instead of scattered details, rounder slots, and every screen in harmony: hence the leaf pattern, the rounded slots and the greyed screens. A second review (same day) asked for the polish of Dark Coffee GUI without its pixels: no names drawn in textures, slimmer arrows, no light line over the slots, a tighter pattern at the same spacing everywhere, cast shadows, rounded slots, a textured well behind the player, a hotbar with rounded ends, and mods' own drawings left untouched. A third review asked for no dark line over the slots, rounded squares instead of round slots, and a finer well behind the player than the grid: the slots now take Dark Coffee's construction (outline, inner shadow, corner profile) redrawn in Fallow's colours, and the well its fine grain. A fourth review asked that nothing be cut or nearly overlap, and that the bars come down into the space of the XP bar. A fifth review asked for the crafting table's corners and curls on every screen, one leaf pattern that does not move with the slots, and a full grain behind the player without the shadow at its top. A sixth review asked for the mods' arrows, the stray light marks around the player, straight crosses between slots, and the crafting table's frame on the other crafting grids. A last full audit before release (vanilla screens, sprites, every mod screen, code and packaging) then fixed the sprites left in GUI Revision's greys and colours, the brewing stand's arrow, the enchanting table's frame, the hopper's open edge, leftovers of old arrows and wells in mod screens, the textures no version reads, the licences of recoloured mod textures, and added the crafter's screen and a pack icon. The menus were then brought to the same tones (shading, separators, a misty forest panorama), the widgets were redrawn (the hover state was darker than the rest state, and the lists' arrows turned green), and FancyMenu and Drippy were added for a Fallow title and loading screen. A review against every texture of Dark Coffee GUI (studied for its ideas, none of its pixels used) then brought the game's titles back (removing them was pointless, since the game translates them), in moss green; leaves and seals under the arrows and pluses, grooves around slots used together, a fire of its own for each furnace, the inventory's lost arrow, selected states in lantern light, the advancements' frame shapes and the sprites still in the game's colours; and gave the menus a leaf veil, riveted buttons filled with lantern light on hover and a veil on the title screen. A last review found the titles at the game's positions fell on the frames and ornaments, and the plus and the arrows off their medallions' centre: the titles moved onto a plaque drawn by the `fallow` mod, the medallions became flat ovals and seals centred on their mark to the pixel, and the anvil's and cartography table's spike became a true plus. The next review found the plaque and the plain medallions too simple, the curls one ornament too many once the titles were back, the corner ornaments missing on some screens and close to the beacon's buttons, and the furnaces' fires unfinished: the titles came back into the panels on plates (or bands) drawn by the pack, the `titres` module was removed, the arrows and pluses took lozenges after Dark Coffee GUI's idea, the corners took four families from GUI Revision with the all-or-small rule, each furnace a finished fire, the menus' veil lost its leaves, and the logo its new stone, moss, vines and lantern.
