# Brumaire, the house shader pack

Living page of the shader pack shipped in `pack/shaderpacks/Brumaire/` (decisions [0028](../decisions/0028-house-shader.md) and [0031](../decisions/0031-brumaire-is-the-look.md)). It is the look of the pack. "Brumaire" is a working name.

## Turning it on

Brumaire is **on from the first launch** (`config/iris.properties`, written once by Default Options). In game: K turns it off or on, O lists the shader packs, R reloads it; Options > Video Settings > Shader Packs holds its settings and profiles. Iris keeps a player's settings in `shaderpacks/Brumaire.txt`. Iris does not support Apple Silicon Macs: there the pack is played without shader, with the vanilla look and the mist of ATMOSPHERICS.

## How a frame is built

| Step | Programs | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Shadow map | `shadow`, `clrwl_shadow` | Sun or moon view, bent so the centre is finer; plants wave as in the main view; water casts no shadow but marks itself (`shadowcolor0`), so the bottom knows it lies under water; stained glass casts no shadow either but leaves the colour it gives the light (`shadowcolor1`) |
| Coloured light, shelter | `shadow`, `shadowcomp.csh`, `shadowcomp1.csh` (compute) | The shadow pass also files every terrain block within 64 blocks horizontally and 32 vertically into a 128×64×128 volume (`voxelImg`): solid, letting light through, or glowing with its colour; the compute pass spreads the light one block per frame, like the game's light, into two volumes used in turn (`lightImgA`, `lightImgB`); a second compute pass finds in each column the top of what stops the rain (`shelterImg`) |
| Sky table | `prepare.csh` (compute) | The light scattered by the atmosphere in every direction around the sun, lit by the sun and the moon, in a 192×108 table; its last row holds the mean light of the sky and what the air leaves of the sun and the moon |
| Footprints | `prepare1.csh` (compute) | Each of the four living beings nearest the camera (`brumairePush0..3`) sets a foot every 0.7 block, left then right, into a 1024×1024 map at 1/16 block that wraps every 64 blocks (`trackImg`); `trackStateImg` keeps where each set its last foot |
| Wakes | `prepare2.csh`, `prepare3.csh` (compute) | A damped wave equation on a 512×512 map at 1/8 block that wraps every 64 blocks, two half steps per frame (`wakeImgA` to `wakeImgB` and back): height, speed and foam; the water is hollowed under each boat, swimmer or wading animal (`brumaireWake0..5`) and given back as it moves on, so the wave equation turns the released hollow into rings when still and a V wake with foam when moving; steep ripples froth; reset flat far from the camera |
| Geometry | `gbuffers_*`, `dh_terrain`, `dh_water`, `clrwl_gbuffers` | Every surface is lit where it is drawn: sun or moon with soft shadows, sky light, block light, the game's ambient occlusion; solid surfaces also write their normal and their share of indirect light (`colortex1`) |
| Screen occlusion | `deferred` (half size), `deferred1` | Hollows, foliage and the foot of walls receive less sky light; smoothed without bleeding across edges; wet ground and puddles take their reflection; then the sky and a round sun are painted behind the terrain, the moon and stars of the game (and of mods) kept; the finished opaque scene is copied to `colortex6` for the water |
| Water | `gbuffers_water` | The surface is drawn whole where it lies: the bottom found at the end of the refracted ray (read in `colortex6`), dimmed along that path while the water scatters light into it, the banks and the sky mirrored in it, blurred where the ripples are finer than the pixel, the sun's glint and the foam |
| Clouds | `composite` (half size) | Volumetric clouds marched through a 3D noise computed once at load (`setup.csh`) |
| Air and mist | `composite1` | The far landscape takes the colour of the low sky, the mist, the light shafts read in the shadow map, the rain curtains beyond the game's drops, the water seen from below |
| Eye and lens | `composite2_a.csh` (compute) | Once per frame: the log-average brightness of the scene (centre-weighted) that the eye slowly adapts to, and the distance of what the centre of the screen looks at, both kept in `lensImg` (one texel) |
| Grade | `composite2` | Depth of field, eye adaptation, glow of the bright lights, night vision, vignette, tone curve, grade, into an 8-bit image (`colortex7`) |
| Temporal anti-aliasing | `composite3` | The image blended with its history (`colortex10`), brought back to where each point stood in the previous frame |
| Motion blur | `composite4` | The image streaked along the path each point took on screen since the previous frame |
| Anti-aliasing | `composite5`, `composite6`, `final` | SMAA: the edges of shapes (`colortex8`), their blending weights (`colortex9`), then the blend with the neighbours on screen |

All the geometry programs include `program/solid.vsh` and `program/solid.fsh` with one `PROGRAM_*` define each; the top-level files only hold that define.

## Sky, light and air

- **The sky is physical**: air scatters blue (Rayleigh), fine haze scatters around the sun (Mie), ozone keeps the blue of the zenith at twilight. The sunset reddens by itself; the sun and the moon light the same atmosphere, the moon by its phase. A summer sky is never deep blue.
- **The light follows the sky**: the colour of the sun is what the air leaves of it (white at noon, orange then red near the horizon); the light of the sky is its mean over the vault; at night the eye sees blue and the night never goes fully black.
- **The air of the biome** (Iris' `rainfall` and `temperature` of the player's biome, eased over about ten seconds): a humid biome has a paler sky, more haze in the distance and more mist, olive in a swamp or a jungle; a cold one has a bluish mist; a hot dry one has dust, a yellowed sky and less mist.
- **Distant air** (`AIR_DENSITY`): the far landscape fades toward the low sky, blue first, into a warm grey rather than a pure sky blue.
- **Distant shadows** (`FAR_SHADOWS`: off, 16, 24 or 32 steps; off in the Very light profile): beyond the shadow map, hills, cliffs and forests still shade the far landscape, Distant Horizons included. Each vertex of the terrain the camera sees (`gbuffers_terrain` and `dh_terrain`) writes into `farHeightImg` the highest surface seen in each column of two blocks; the map covers 4,096 blocks around the player and keeps what it has seen when the view turns away (each cell carries the rest of its position, so a cell taken over by a nearer column is recognised). A far point then climbs toward the sun over that map, with steps that lengthen up to 512 blocks, and is shaded where the relief passes in front, with a penumbra that widens with the distance of what hides it. Plants and vines are left out of the map. The map only keeps the highest surface seen: a tree cut far away keeps its shadow until the player has gone some 2,000 blocks away.
- **Light in the air** (`LIGHT_SHAFTS`, `SHAFT_STRENGTH`): the air itself scatters a little sun, mostly forward; where foliage or a ridge cuts it in the shadow map, shafts. Only in the open.
- **Dust in the light** (`AIR_MOTES`): specks of one block pixel float in about one block in three within 12 blocks; only those the shadow map says the sun reaches are drawn, warm with the light, faint with the sun behind and bright against it, and only against a dark background: in front of the sky or sunlit ground a speck is lost (the background is read in the shadow map), except straight against the sun. A beam through a window or a gap in the canopy shows its dust; the shade and the night show none. Off in the rain and in `Très léger`.
- **Fireflies** (`FIREFLIES`, needs `SHADER_RAIN` for the ground; off in Very light): on mild nights in warm, damp biomes (biome temperature about 0.5 to 1.2, rainfall above 0.3), with no rain or snow and under open sky, the fog pass marches cells of two blocks within 40 blocks; about one in eight holds a firefly that drifts slowly in its cell, between half a block and three blocks above the ground of its column (the rain shelter map), and flashes yellow-green every few seconds: a one-pixel core and a soft halo the bloom spreads.
- **Cave drips** (`CAVE_DRIPS`, needs `COLORED_LIGHT`; off in Very light): underground, about one column in twenty within 16 blocks, under a ceiling at most twelve blocks up and over a floor, drips every two to five seconds: the drop hangs half a second under the ceiling, then falls with gravity, two pixels long. It is only seen by the torch, lava or lamp light around it (the coloured light volume), like the dust underground.
- **The eye adapts**: the image brightens at night (up to 2.2×) and underground (1.5×).
- **Soft shadows** (`SHADOW_SOFTNESS`, `SHADOW_SEARCH`): the shader first looks how far the shadow caster is (samples near the centre weigh more, so a distant edge does not blur a crisp shadow), and widens the edge with that distance, as under a tall tree.
- **Pixel shadows** (`PIXEL_SHADOWS`): within 40 blocks, on the axis-aligned faces of the terrain, each texel reads the shadow at its own centre (the point snapped to the 1/16 grid on the face) with a noise drawn from the texel instead of the screen pixel, so a shadow edge steps texel by texel like the rest of the drawing.
- **Light through foliage** (`LEAF_TRANSLUCENCY`): the same search tells how much foliage stands between a leaf and the sun; the light that passes is fainter for each block crossed and tinted by the green of the leaves it went through, so a thin crown glows and a dense one stays dark inside. Against the light, leaves glow green, more where the canopy in front is thin.
- **Contact shadows** (`CONTACT_SHADOWS`, `deferred1`): within 48 blocks a short trace toward the sun on screen finds the small things the shadow map is too coarse for (grass, ledges, slabs, objects on a table); only the direct part of the light is darkened.
- **Sky light from a hemisphere**: faces turned up get the zenith, faces turned sideways the paler horizon, faces turned down the sun sent back by the ground (a warm green-brown), instead of one ambient colour scaled by the normal.
- **Light bounce** (`SSGI`, `SSGI_STRENGTH`, `deferred`): from each pixel, a few rays drawn by the cosine of the surface are walked on screen with lengthening steps over some four blocks; the first surface they meet sends back the light it shows in the lit image, received by the colour of the surface (written by the opaque programs into `colortex4`). A sunlit red floor turns the foot of a white wall pink, a sunlit wall brightens the ground in its shade, lava reddens the rock around it. Rays that leave the screen bring nothing back, so the bounce fades at the screen edges and beyond 96 blocks; it is smoothed with the occlusion, without crossing edges or faces.
- **Screen occlusion** (`AO_SAMPLES`, `AO_RADIUS`, `AO_STRENGTH`): only the light of the sky and of blocks is darkened, never the sun; half as much on foliage. Off in the Light profile.
- **Tone curve that keeps the hue**: the curve is applied to the intensity of a colour (a mix of its luminance and its strongest channel) instead of to each channel, so sand, snow or a sunlit field keep their colour instead of bleaching; only what still exceeds white fades to it, like film.
- **Grade** (calibrated on Recrafted screenshots, 2026-11-17): greens pulled toward a warm olive (`OLIVE`), a warm sun and a less blue sky light, shadows that keep their colour (more saturation in the darks than in the lights), a matte image (blacks barely lifted).

## Coloured light

- **Blocks light in their colour** (`COLORED_LIGHT`, off in the Very light profile): torches, lanterns, fire and campfires orange; soul torches, lanterns and fire blue; lava and magma red-orange; glowstone, shroomlight, lamps and ochre froglights warm yellow; sea lanterns, end rods and pearlescent froglights bluish white; verdant froglights and glow lichen green; amethyst, crying obsidian and portals violet; redstone red. Lights of other mods keep the warm colour of a flame.
- **How**: the shadow pass files the terrain around the camera into a volume; a compute pass spreads the light block by block like the game's own (one level lost per block, stopped by solid blocks, through glass, plants and partial blocks). Each cell keeps the colour of its strongest light; two lights of the same strength mix. Only the colour comes from the volume: the brightness stays the game's, which already knows what blocks the light.
- **Shaped block light** (`BLOCK_LIGHT_SHAPE`): the slope of the light level in the volume tells where the light comes from, so faces turned toward a torch get more of it than those turned away; flames (and lights of the flame colour) waver softly, each block on its own rhythm.
- **Light in hand**: what the player holds lights around them in its colour, a little further than a placed block, even without a dynamic-light mod; with LambDynamicLights the brighter of the two wins, never both.
- Beyond the volume (64 blocks around, Distant Horizons terrain) block light keeps the warm colour of a flame.
- **Stained glass light** (`GLASS_TINT`, off in the Very light profile): sunlight and moonlight that pass stained glass take its colour, on what they light and in the shafts of light in the air; the more opaque the pane, the purer the colour, and dark or tinted glass holds most of the light back. Read from the shadow map, so it reaches as far as the sun's shadows. It needs Iris Veil Compat 0.3.0: 0.4.0 leaves water and stained glass out of the shadow map.
- **Connected glass** (`CONNECTED_GLASS`, `lib/glass.glsl`): the shadow pass marks glass blocks and panes in the block volume (`VOX_GLASS`). On a glass face, a texel within 1/16 of an edge looks at the block across that edge: if it is glass too, the frame texel is dropped (clear glass, whose sprite is mostly transparent) or replaced by the sprite's mean colour (stained and tinted glass); a corner texel goes only if the glass also continues diagonally. A glass wall reads as one pane. Beyond the block volume (64 blocks around the camera) the frames stay.

## Clouds

- **One layer, several kinds**: from `CLOUD_HEIGHT` (220) to 90 blocks above, shaped by a 64³ noise that repeats without seams (smooth noise hollowed by cells for the shape, finer cells for the frayed edges), computed once when the pack loads. A slow weather map drifting with the wind decides, place by place, how covered the sky is and which kind grows there (`CLOUD_TYPES`): flat stratocumulus banks that stay low and even, round cumulus that billow up to two thirds of the layer, and under thunder towers that reach the top of the layer and spread into anvils. Light rain flattens them into a grey sheet, thunder raises them.
- **Shape**: flat bases, a sharp threshold so cumulus have clear edges with blue sky between. The fine cells, twisted by a slow swirl so their grid never shows, fray the bottoms and make cauliflower tops (the pattern is inverted with height). The upper wind pushes the top of the layer further than its base: towers lean downwind.
- **Light** (`CLOUD_LIGHT_STEPS`, `CLOUD_MULTISCATTER`): from each sample a cone of steps that double in length is walked toward the sun or the moon (fine detail on the first two), plus one look far ahead so a tower shades its neighbour. Multiple scattering is approximated by octaves, each less extinguished, less forward and weaker than the one before, so light glows through the heart of a cumulus instead of leaving it black. The droplet phase has a narrow forward peak (silver lining against the sun), a wide lobe and a backward lobe. The powder term darkens thin edges seen against the sun. The sky lights the tops more than the bellies, less under another cloud; the sunlit ground sends a warm-green bounce under the bellies.
- **Lightning** (`CLOUD_LIGHTNING`): a strike lights the column of cloud it crosses; under thunder, sheet lightning flickers in random cells of 400 blocks.
- **Aerial perspective**: far clouds fade into the low sky at the distance where they are actually seen (mean of the march weighted by what is taken), not where the ray enters the layer.
- **Altocumulus** (`CLOUD_ALTO`): 340 blocks above the layer, a thin sheet of small rounded cells (the dappled sky) cut from the fine-cell noise inside banks that come and go over hours, more in humid weather and before rain. Each cell has a shaded side away from the sun (the sheet is read again a few blocks toward the light), and thin edges light up against it. It dims the sun on the ground in soft patches. Drawn between the main layer and the cirrus.
- **Cirrus** (`CLOUD_CIRRUS`): 760 blocks above the layer, a thin veil of ice streaks stretched along the upper wind (three octaves, the fine pulled by the coarse), coming and going over hours, more in humid weather and before rain. Ice scatters strongly forward: bright near the sun, barely brighter than the sky away from it, pink at sunset. It softens the sun on the ground a little.
- Pushed by the pack's wind; the coverage (`CLOUD_COVERAGE`) grows in humid biomes and in the rain, where they turn grey. The mist ring still closes the horizon.
- Their shadows pass over the ground (`CLOUD_SHADOWS`).
- The game's clouds are not drawn; with `CLOUD_STEPS` at 0 they come back.

## Sky optics and night sky

- **Ice halos** (`SKY_HALO`, `lib/skyoptics.glsl`): where cirrus crosses the sun or the moon, the 22° halo (red inside, sharp inner edge, fading outward) and a fainter 46° halo; with a low light, sun dogs at the same height left and right of it, a little beyond 22°.
- **Rainbow** (`SKY_RAINBOW`): around the antisolar point when the ground is still wet (`wetness`) and the rain has almost gone, with the sun low enough for the bow to stand above the horizon: the primary bow (40.5° to 42.4°, red outside), the secondary (50° to 53.5°, reversed, about a third as bright), a darker Alexander band between them and a brighter sky under the primary. It shows in patches, stronger low down where rain still falls.
- **Stars** (`SKY_STARS`, `lib/nightsky.glsl`): three layers drawn on a cube around the sky, each cell holding at most one star at a random place, with a brightness drawn from a steep law (a few bright, many faint) and a colour from orange to blue-white. They are fixed in a frame that turns with the sun around the celestial pole (tilted by `sunPathRotation`), so the night sky rotates. They twinkle, more near the horizon, and fade through the thicker air there. The game's stars are no longer drawn; at `Du jeu` they come back.
- **Milky Way** (`SKY_MILKY_WAY`): a band tilted across the celestial equator with a ragged width, a warmer bulge toward its centre, grain from unresolved stars, dark dust lanes slightly off its middle, and more faint stars along it. A full moon pales it and drowns the faintest stars.
- **Shooting stars** (`SKY_METEORS`): two independent lanes, one every 7 s and one every 11 s, each with a 45 % chance of firing; a bright head with a fading tail crosses part of the sky in about a second, dimmed like the stars by the haze near the horizon.
- **Northern lights** (`SKY_AURORA`, `SKY_AURORA_STRENGTH`): curtains drawn by marching 14 layers through a band of altitude above the player, a bent ribbon with vertical rays that shimmer slowly, green at the base and violet at the top. They only show at night, in cold biomes (`airHeat` under about 0.35), and on one night out of four or so, drawn from `worldDay`, so they stay an event.
- **Moon aureole**: haze scatters a soft glow around the moon, wider in humid biomes and in rain.
- Clouds and cirrus are laid over the stars and the bows (front to back: the bow, the cloud layer, the cirrus veil with its halos).

## Wind

- Grass, flowers, crops, leaves and vines move with the pack's wind (`vent` module of the `fallow` mod): a slow swell travelling downwind and a quicker quiver, stronger in the gusts that cross the landscape (`WIND_STRENGTH`). Sheltered plants (little sky light) stay still.
- Grass and flowers lie down and part around the feet of the four living beings nearest the camera, the player included, even out of the wind.
- Leaves brushed by one of those beings (walking along a hedge, standing on a canopy) lean away from it and quiver quickly while it stays against them, even out of the wind. Falling leaves stay Particular's particles: textured, they read better than leaves drawn by the shader, which were tried and dropped.
- **Grass sheen** (`FOLIAGE_SHEEN`): each vertex of grass, flowers and leaves passes how far it is bent, relative to what its plant can bend (`vBend`), to the fragment. A bent blade shows its paler, yellower underside, more toward the tip, so the gusts cross a meadow in light waves and a trampled path stays paler.
- **Fluttering leaves** (`LEAF_FLUTTER`, off in Very light): in the wind, about one leaf texel in eight to twelve lights up for an instant with the sun's colour and goes out, each at its own pace; they turn more often and brighter in strong wind, never in the shade.
- **Bushy leaves** (`BUSHY_LEAVES`, `BUSHY_DISTANCE` 32 to 128 blocks; on in the Balanced and Beautiful profiles, off in the two light ones): cutout terrain (leaves, grass, flowers) goes through its own program, `gbuffers_terrain_cutout`, whose geometry shader (`program/bushy.gsh`) passes every triangle through and gives each leaf face within range two more planes of the same texture: a little larger than the face, lifted off it, shifted and tilted at random per face, so both triangles of a face draw the same planes. Tree outlines stop reading as stacked cubes and the foliage gains depth. With the setting off, the program is disabled and cutout terrain falls back to `gbuffers_terrain` with no geometry shader, so it costs nothing. The extra planes cast no shadow of their own. Cost on a real graphics card is not measured yet (the test bench renders in software).
- Rain and snow slant with the wind, more in a storm.
- Without the mod, a steady fallback wind, stronger in the rain.

## Water

- **The waves** are a spectrum of Gerstner wave trains in three bands (`lib/waves.glsl`): a swell of 6 to 20 blocks, wind waves of 1.2 to 6 blocks and ripples of a quarter block to one block (`WATER_WAVE_COUNT` trains, 8 to 28, shared a quarter, a half and a quarter). Each train follows the deep-water dispersion (a long wave travels faster) and its crest wavers along its length. The directions are fixed once and spread around the circle; the wind only gives more height to the trains that go its way (narrowly for the swell, loosely for the ripples), so nothing slides when the wind turns. The swell needs open water: eight rays walk the water around each point through the block volume, and a pond enclosed by its banks keeps only the wind waves (`WATER_SWELL`). Waves flatten over shallows. The Gerstner displacement gathers the water under the crests and sharpens them (`WATER_CHOP`); the Jacobian of that displacement tells where the surface folds over. Trains finer than the pixel fade out and their slope becomes roughness, so distant water blurs instead of shimmering. `WATER_WAVES` scales them all. With `WATER_PIXELS` the waves are read on the 1/16 block grid of the textures and their slope goes in steps: the reflections break into hard-edged patches, like a drawn texture.
- **Current** (`WATER_FLOW`): the slope of a flowing water face gives the downhill direction; the wave pattern is carried along it in two phases half a period apart that take turns, so it flows forever without stretching, and the ripples line up with the current. Fast water foams.
- **What shows through**: the refracted ray is followed on screen down to the bottom (`WATER_REFRACTION` steps then five halvings; 0 falls back to a cheap offset), so the bottom shifts like through real water and nothing above the surface (a boat, a plant) leaks into it. Along that path the light of the bottom is dimmed (red first) and the water adds the light it scatters itself, integrated exactly for one scattering: the sun that went down through the surface, more toward the sun (forward scattering) and less looking straight down, and the sky light. The colour is the biome's pulled toward a grey-green (`WATER_MURK`). Shallows show the bottom, a deep river shows its body; looking toward a low sun the crests glow green where the light passes through them.
- **What it mirrors**: exact Fresnel for water (a little boosted, the water reads first by its reflections). The reflected ray is followed on screen until it meets the banks, the trees or a boat (`WATER_REFLECTIONS` steps; what lies under the surface cannot be mirrored). The crossing point is refined, then checked: a ray that passed behind a trunk or a bank without touching it goes on instead of stretching that object downward. Where the screen does not show what is mirrored (the bank behind the player, a tree above the frame, something hidden), the ray goes on block by block through the 128 × 64 × 128 volume around the camera that the shadow pass fills for the coloured light, and the first block it meets gives its average colour (top or side, kept by the shadow pass from the smallest mip of its texture, biome tint included), lit by the sun with its shadow, the sky unless a block covers it within eight blocks and the coloured light around it; a light block glows in the reflection (`VOXEL_REFLECTIONS` blocks, also for ice and puddles). Only masses and colours, no texture detail, which is enough at the distance where the screen no longer shows the thing; small plants and torches are left out. Beyond the volume, the sky with its mist and the clouds at mid-height of their layer. Where the ripples are finer than the pixel, or under rain, each frame mirrors along a microfacet drawn from a GGX distribution of that roughness, and the anti-aliasing averages them into a blurred reflection (`WATER_BLUR`).
- **The sun's glint** is a GGX microfacet highlight, never narrower than the sun's disc, cut by shadows and clouds and dulled by rain: a path of glitter toward a low sun, hard-edged plates with `WATER_PIXELS`.
- **Wakes and foam**: what crosses the water ripples it (`WATER_WAKES`): a boat, a swimmer or an animal wading in pushes the surface down, still it leaves small rings, moving a wake whose ripples bend the reflections and a trail of foam that fades in a few seconds; ripples that break also froth. Along the banks (`WATER_FOAM`, solid blocks at water level found in the block volume, within two blocks) a fringe breathes against the shore and bands of foam roll in from open water and die on it, stronger with wind and swell. Under a waterfall the water churns: foam and rings around where it lands (`WATER_FALLS`). In strong wind or storms on open water, crests where the surface folds over break into whitecaps, and the foam trails behind them for a second or two (`WATER_WHITECAPS`). All foam is drawn as a lace of cells that opens when there is little and closes into a sheet when there is much, on the pixel grid with `WATER_PIXELS`; it takes the daylight and the sky, without reflection.
- **Rain on water** (`WATER_RAIN_RINGS`): drops ring the surface at two sizes and flick a dot of foam where they hit.
- **Waterfalls**: a side face of water with water above it (the block volume marks water, bit 12) is falling water: thin, whitened by the air it carries, streaked by dashes that run down each 1/16 column at their own speed. The edge of water that merely flows only trembles.
- **Under the surface**: the sun reaches the bottom weakened and tinted by the water above it (read in the shadow map). The caustics come from the waves themselves: the light of a patch of surface lands on a patch deformed by the surface's curvature, and its brightness is divided by the area; the strength is set from the average curvature so the threads close up from the first block of water, softer on calm water (`WATER_CAUSTICS`). Red and blue bend a little differently: the edges of the threads catch a rainbow (`WATER_DISPERSION`).
- **From below**: the world above shows through a circle (Snell's window), bent by the waves, its edge darkening; beyond the critical angle the surface reflects everything (total internal reflection) and mirrors the water and the bottom. The view under water scatters the same light as from above along the eye's path, a little further, brighter toward the surface and the sun; it stays clear near the surface and darkens with each block of water over the eye (the sky light at the eye).
- **Diving**: rays come down from the surface, slanted by refraction, cut by the shadow of what floats or overhangs and rippling with the caustics, brightest when looking toward the light (`LIGHT_SHAFTS`, `SHAFT_STRENGTH`). Suspended specks of one block pixel (marine snow) drift slowly in about one block in three within 20 blocks and twinkle, brighter against the light (`WATER_MOTES`).
- Distant Horizons water uses the same scattering, Fresnel and sky reflection, and since its waves are all finer than the pixel their whole slope becomes roughness: a wide path of light under the sun.

## Rain

- **The drops** of the game are drawn as thin light-grey streaks lit by the sky, fainter than the game's blue ones and fading right in front of the eye; snow keeps its flakes.
- **Wet ground**: where it rains (the biome under the player has rain, not snow, read through `biome_precipitation`) and a surface sees the sky, it darkens and shines at a low angle (`WETNESS`). Iris smooths the rain into `wetness`: the ground soaks in about a minute and dries in a few minutes after the rain stops (`drynessHalfLife` is set to one minute, six times the default, so that the drying patches can be seen). Leaves, plants and glowing blocks stay as they are.
- **The grain of wet ground** (`PATCHY_DRYING`): each texel is compared with the mean colour of its texture (a far mipmap level of the atlas). Darker texels are hollows: they soak and darken the most. Brighter ones are bumps: they darken less and shine more. When the rain stops, the ground dries in patches on the 1/16 grid (a wide pattern of the cloud noise, a grain per texel and the relief), the bumps and exposed places first, the hollows and the foot of walls last. While it rains hard, everything is soaked.
- **Rivulets on walls** (`WALL_RIVULETS`): on vertical faces that see the sky, about one pixel column in six carries a dark wet line for two blocks, then jumps aside or stops. Now and then a bright drop slides down it, one pixel at a time, with a fading wet trail behind.
- **Rain on windows** (`WINDOW_RAIN`; off in Very light): glass and panes open to the sky take one-pixel beads that land, dry and come back elsewhere in their 3×3 cell, and on upright faces about one pixel column in ten carries a drop that runs down fast with a short wet trail. The water lightens and thickens the glass where it sits (plain glass is cut out, so a bead reads as a pixel of water).
- **Wet creatures** (`WET_ENTITIES`; off in Very light): out in the rain under open sky (`rainSoak`, a few seconds to soak, about forty to dry), animals, armour and the player's arm darken, saturate a little and shine (gloss and a smoother surface for the sun's highlight); coming out of water (`swimSoak`) the player's arm streams for half a minute.
- **Splashes** (`RAIN_SPLASHES`): on wet flat ground, drops burst on the 1/16 grid: one bright pixel, then a crown of four pixels that fades, in a few hundredths of a second, more often in heavy rain.
- **Puddles** gather on flat ground in patches with hard edges on the 1/16 block grid, larger as the ground soaks (`PUDDLES`, `PUDDLE_AMOUNT`). They mirror the landscape like the water, on screen (`WET_REFLECTIONS` steps) or the sky, and the drops draw rings on them. The terrain marks them in the alpha of `colortex1`; `deferred1` draws their reflection.
- **Rain curtains**: beyond the game's drops, thin falling streaks over the distant landscape, leaning with the wind (`RAIN_CURTAINS`).
- **The air** under rain turns a slightly warm grey and the mist thickens a little.
- **Lightning**: the bolt (`gbuffers_lightning`) is a bright blue-white line that glows; while it strikes, the sky and the whole landscape flash, less when it falls far away (`lightningBoltPosition`).

### Rain and snow drawn by the shader

- **Drops and flakes** (`SHADER_RAIN`, off in the Very light profile, where the game's rain stays): the game's rain and snow are hidden and the shader draws its own in `composite1` (`lib/precip.glsl`): one drop or flake per column of three quarters of a block, found by walking the columns the view crosses up to 26 blocks; drops fall on the sixteenth-of-a-block grid like the pixels of the textures, one pixel wide, lit by the sky; flakes are pixel squares that drift. The wind slants them.
- **Shelter**: a compute pass (`shadowcomp1`, `program/shelter.csh`) finds in the coloured-light volume, for each column, the top of the highest block that stops rain (everything but grass, flowers, vines and torches); no drop falls below it, so nothing falls under a roof or a dense tree.
- **Showers**: patches of about a hundred blocks pushed by the wind: the rain grows heavier and lighter as they pass, and the far rain curtains are thicker where it rains hard.
- In a snowy biome (the player's), flakes instead of drops.
- **Drops of different sizes**: rain streaks have different lengths; flakes are one or two pixels wide, and the larger ones tumble (flat, then edge-on).
- **Light in the drops** (`PRECIP_GLOW`): drops and flakes within 16 blocks read the coloured light volume, so near a torch or a lantern they take its colour. Each drop is a small lens: toward the sun or the moon it sparkles (a narrow forward lobe), a flake scatters wider and softer.
- **Blizzard** (`SNOW_BLIZZARD`): under a thunderstorm in a snowy biome, the wind lays the flakes down (five times the slant), they fall faster, denser and stretched into short streaks.

## Snow, sand and ice

- **Snow on trees and ledges**: while the player stands in a biome where it snows (`biome_precipitation` 2, smoothed over ten seconds into `snowHere`), the tops of blocks and leaves that see the sky turn white on the 1/16 grid. On leaves the snow holds on the bright needles and leaves the dark hollows, and a band of three to five pixels runs down their sides with a ragged lower edge (`SNOW_COVER`). The whole view follows the player's biome: a forest seen from a snowy biome is frosted too.
- **Laid snow as a material** (`SNOW_DETAIL`): snow on the top of blocks is no longer a flat white plate. Where the cover is partial (under trees, where the sky is half hidden) it lies in patches on the 1/16 grid that join as the cover grows, and it melts in a ring around torches and lanterns. Its height mixes drifts a few blocks wide, ripples drawn across the wind (the mod's wind when present) and a grain per texel; the sun follows that slope, the hollows are slightly greyer. In the shade the snow takes the blue of the sky that lights it alone, and its edges glow faintly against the light. Glints are tiny ice facets drawn by lot, one per chosen texel, each with its own tilt: they flash only when the eye stands in their reflection, so they move as the player walks. Vanilla snow layers and blocks get the same slope, shade and glints over their own texture. Beyond about 30 blocks the slope fades out.
- **Snow that builds up** (`SNOW_BUILDUP`): falling snow where the player stands (`rainStrength` in a snowy biome) is accumulated into `snowStorm`, which climbs over a few minutes of snowfall and sinks back over some twenty-five minutes after it. While it is high, the cover creeps further under trees and overhangs, more needles hold snow and the band down the sides of leaves holds on more needles; trunks, walls and rocks facing the wind are plastered in patches on the 1/16 grid, more toward the top of each block and on the bright texels first.
- **Snow** blocks are dimmed a little, so the snow keeps its grain in full sun instead of burning white; a few grains glitter where the sun reaches, and change from time to time.
- **Sand** keeps its warm colour in the sun (darker and slightly richer than the game's texture, otherwise it reads as snow) and glitters more sparsely.
- **Wind-rippled sand** (`SAND_DETAIL`): within about 30 blocks, the top of sand takes ripples across the wind (the mod's wind when present), about a third of a block apart, gentle on the windward side and steep behind, twisted by slow noise and fading out in places, over broad undulations. Their slope, stepped on the 1/16 grid, takes the sun (the same path as the snow's slope), crests are a little lighter and the steep side a little darker. Rain flattens them.
- **Cracked ice** (`ICE_DETAIL`): on clear, packed and blue ice, white cracks follow the edges of cells about a block and a half wide, broken in places, with a few short fine fractures and single bubbles; on clear ice they are opaque white while the rest stays see-through.
- **Falling leaves** (`FALLING_LEAVES`; off in the Very light profile): within 16 blocks, now and then a leaf comes off the trees and drifts down, swaying, turning edge-on and pushed by the wind, as an L of three texels in the colour of the foliage it falls from. A compute pass (`shadowcomp2`, `program/canopy.csh`) reads the block volume and stores, for each column, the underside of the highest foliage and its colour (`canopyImg`); leaves only fall up to ten blocks below a canopy, or up to two blocks past its edge. Foliage turned red or orange in autumn sheds more. The fog pass marches the cells along each ray, so the leaf is hidden by whatever stands in front of it.
- **Ice**: clear ice is drawn with the water, light blue, with the sky and the landscape mirrored in it, more at a low angle; packed and blue ice shine like a tarnished mirror (screen reflection, no ripples).
- **Frost** (`FROST`): in a cool biome (taiga, windswept hills; barely in the plains, never in a warm one) on a clear night, grass blocks, grass, flowers and leaves that see the sky take a pale veil and a rime on their brightest texels, about a third of them at the coldest, following the drawing of the texture on the 1/16 grid. It forms from dusk, holds at sunrise and melts by mid-morning; none under rain, where snow already covers, or near a light.
- **Dew** (`DEW`; off in Very light): the warm counterpart of frost. On mild mornings in warm, damp biomes, from sunrise to mid-morning, grass block tops, grass and flowers that see the sky take scattered glints (the snow's facets, which follow the eye) and a slight wet sheen; none under rain, snow or frost.
- **Footprints**: the player and the animals nearest to them leave a print every 0.7 block, left then right, on sand, snow blocks, snow layers and snow-covered ground. A print is a sole four pixels wide on the 1/16 grid, wider at the front than at the heel, turned in the walking direction and sized by the being's width. Its hollow is darker (bluish on snow, brown on sand) and its edges tilt the normal, so one side catches the light and the other falls in shade. A print records the foot's height, so a flying player or a block placed on top shows none. Prints fade over `TRACK_MINUTES` (4 by default), faster under rain or falling snow; the map covers 64 blocks around the camera, so a trail left farther away is forgotten when the player comes back (`TRACKS`).
- **Wet prints**: a being that walks out of water (`brumaireWet`, from the `fallow` mod) leaves wet soles on any ground, sand included, snow and ice excepted: darker and slightly glossy, fewer and fainter as its feet dry over some 24 blocks. Each print dries in about two minutes, faster in the sun, from its edges inward. The wetness is kept in the print's texel (`a` = tile + 256 × wetness 0..6). Under rain the ground is already wet and the prints are not drawn.
- **Heat shimmer**: in a dry, hot biome by day, the air near the horizon trembles a few pixels, more with distance (`HEAT_SHIMMER`). A desert also clears the near mist.

## The finished image

- **Eye adaptation** (`EXPOSURE_AUTO`, off in Very light; `EXPOSURE_AUTO_AMOUNT`, `EXPOSURE_SPEED`): `lens.csh` measures the log-average brightness of a 32×18 grid (each point a small cross, the centre weighted more) and follows it over time, in about a second toward light and several seconds toward darkness. The grade opens a dark scene and closes a dazzling one, partially only (a power of 0.3, clamped between 0.75 and 1.25), on top of the former day, night and underground adaptation; a typical forest by day is unchanged, a bright panorama a little darker, a cave a little brighter.
- **Glow** (`BLOOM`): five levels of the mipmap chain, from a tight core to a wide trail; a soft-knee threshold, so lights approaching the threshold already glow a little, and the threshold follows the eye's adaptation.
- **Sun glare** (`SUN_GLARE`, `SUN_GLARE_STRENGTH`): looking toward the sun, thin rays (six main branches and fainter ones), a tight veil and a wide one, and four pale coloured lens ghosts on the line from the sun through the centre of the image. All of it scales with the sun as the image actually shows it: a blurred mip level around the sun minus the sky around it, so a cloud, a tree or a hill puts it out. It fades out a few steps beyond the screen edge.
- **Chromatic aberration** (`CHROMATIC_ABERRATION`): red slides outward and blue inward toward the edges, about a pixel in the corners at 720p.
- **Lens drops** (`LENS_DROPS`; off in the Very light profile): out in the rain under open sky (`lensWet`: rain at the player and sky light of at least 200, a few seconds to wet, about twenty to dry), drops land on the picture in three sizes, the small ones more common, fewer when looking at the ground. Each one lives a few seconds, shrinks as it dries and gives way to another elsewhere in its cell; the large ones slide down slowly. A drop is a small lens: it shows the scene upside down and tighter, slightly blurred, with a darker rim, a lighter crescent at the bottom and a highlight at the top. Coming out of water (`lensDive`), the picture streams for about six seconds. Drawn after the depth of field, so drops stay sharp.
- **Cold breath** (`COLD_BREATH`; off in Very light): in bitter cold (biome temperature under about 0.2, or where it snows) and out of water, every 3.6 seconds a pale puff leaves the bottom of the picture, rises and opens over 1.4 seconds and fades. It scatters the ambient light (the eye's average brightness), so it shows by day and stays faint at night.
- **Canopy drips and snow clumps** (`CANOPY_DRIPS`, needs the block volume; off in Very light): under nearby foliage (the canopy image of `shadowcomp2`), while it rains and as long as the ground stays wet, half of the columns let three drops each fall from the underside of the leaves, one every one to three seconds, falling freely and drawn as a short streak that lengthens with speed. When snow loads the trees and the wind rises, one column in eight drops a clump of snow every six to fifteen seconds, slowed by the air and crumbling as it falls. Drops take the sky light, clumps the sun through the shadow map.
- **Ground drift** (`GROUND_DRIFT`, needs the shader rain; off in Very light): when the pack's wind passes about half force, on snowy ground or in a desert, pale streaks stretched along the wind run over the ground, three quarters of a block thick, sampled only on the end of the ray that grazes the ground. The ground is the rain shelter height, so it stays out of forests.
- **Water steam** (`WATER_STEAM`, needs the block volume; off in Very light): over open water (a water voxel under one or two air cells), pale wisps rise slowly up to two blocks, on cool nights and mornings (biome temperature under about 0.9) and at any hour in bitter cold, not in rain or strong wind. Ten samples up to 56 blocks, on the end of the ray that dips to the water or along a ray that skims it.
- **Lava embers** (`LAVA_EMBERS`, needs the block volume; off in Very light): over lava (a voxel with light 12 or more of the lava family, up to six blocks under the cell), one column in two carries two sparks that live two to three seconds every three to six: they shoot up, slow down and hover up to four blocks, swirl and drift with the wind, cool from yellow to red, flicker and go out. Added as light, so the bloom makes them glow.
- **Waterfall spray** (`FALL_SPRAY`, needs the block volume; off in Very light): falling water is a water voxel under another water voxel; an air cell beside one (one or two blocks away, or one block lower) gets a veil of droplets that rolls and rises, thicker at the foot where water lies below. Fourteen samples up to 28 blocks.
- **Seed fluff** (`SEED_FLUFF`, needs the shader rain; off in Very light): on mild, dry days in the open, one 2-block cell in ten carries a seed tuft between half a block and four blocks above the ground, carried through its cell by the wind and swaying. Lit by the sun through the shadow map, strongly against the light.
- **Eaves drips** (`EAVES_DRIPS`, needs the shader rain; off in Very light): a column whose neighbour is sheltered at least two and a half blocks higher, with air under that neighbour's edge (a roof overhang, a balcony, a rock ledge, not a wall or a cliff), drips during and after rain: three columns in four carry two drops that swell at the edge and fall freely to the ground, every half second in rain and up to every two seconds after. Drawn as streaks lit by the sky.
- **Snow on the lens** (`LENS_SNOW`; off in Very light): out in snowfall (`lensSnow` uniform), six-armed flakes land on the picture in two sizes, melt into round drops that blur the picture slightly, and dry, four to eight seconds each.
- **Frost on the lens** (`LENS_FROST`; off in Very light): out in bitter cold (biome temperature under 0.15) for about a minute (`lensFrost` uniform), straight, branching ice needles creep in from the corners, joined deeper in by a blurred white veil, with glinting points; it melts in a few seconds under shelter or in warmth.
- **Steaming ground** (`SOIL_STEAM`, needs the shader rain; off in Very light): when the ground is still wet, the rain has stopped and the sun is up in a mild biome (temperature 0.45 to 1.8), pale vapour rises up to about a block over sunlit ground (one shadow lookup where the ray lands), sampled only on the end of the ray that grazes the ground; it fades as the wetness dries.
- **Leaves in the wind** (`LEAF_LITTER`, needs the block volume; off in Very light): in autumn (russet canopy above, from `shadowcomp2`) and with the pack's wind above about a quarter of its force, ground cells (air on a solid block) carry dead leaves that cross them along the wind in little hops, flat when sliding and upright in the air, in the foliage colour pulled towards brown.
- **Glowing plankton** (`PLANKTON`, `PLANKTON_STRENGTH`; off in Very light): at night, on warm water (recognised by its blue-green biome tint), the foam of breaking waves, wakes, rain rings and falls glows a cold blue, with brighter blinking specks on the sixteenth-of-a-block grid.
- **Depth of field** (`DOF`: off, soft, photo; soft in the Beautiful profile only): the focus is the nearest of five points around the centre of the screen, followed in about a quarter of a second. Each pixel is blurred by a disc sized by the gap between the inverse distances (a lens's circle of confusion), up to 6 or 12 pixels at 720p, with 24 taps on a golden spiral; a tap only counts if it is itself blurred enough to reach the pixel, so a sharp foreground does not bleed over the background. The hand stays sharp.
- **Night vision** (`PURKINJE`): in the darkest parts of the image, colours fade toward a slightly blue luminance, as rods take over from cones.
- **Vignette** (`VIGNETTE`): the corners darken by up to a fifth.

## Block materials

`block.properties` gives each block a family (its number modulo 32, read everywhere as before) and, for ordinary full blocks, a material (the number divided by 32, read by `lib/material.glsl`). There are 21 materials: polished stone and quartz, glazed terracotta, concrete, iron, gold, fresh, tarnished (exposed and weathered) and oxidised copper, netherite, gem blocks, obsidian, ores, prismarine, amethyst, sculk, terracotta, wool, mud and clay, gilded blackstone, and Create's brass and zinc. Each sets a roughness, a metalness and how much sky deferred1 reflects (`MATERIALS`).

- **The sun's glint**: every lit surface gets a GGX reflection of the sun or moon, scaled by the light that actually reaches it (shadow, clouds, water above and stained glass, exported by `shade()` as `gSunReach`). Rough stone gives a broad faint sheen at a grazing angle, polished stone and gems a sharp spot, metals a glint in their own colour. The glint follows the texel slope when the relief or the snow gives one (`SPECULAR_STRENGTH`).
- **Metals** lose two thirds of their diffuse colour and reflect in it instead: the sky in the reflected direction (darker under a roof, blurrier on rougher metal) and the nearby block light, tinted by their colour, with a Fresnel that whitens less than stone so gold stays golden at a grazing angle. Copper turns from metal to matte as it oxidises.
- **Ores and gilded blackstone** are handled texel by texel: a texel more saturated and brighter than its texture's mean (mip level 4) is mineral and turns smooth and half-metallic (fully metallic gold in gilded blackstone), while the stone around stays matte.
- **Wool** gets a soft sheen at a grazing angle instead of a glint; **sculk** veins (cyan texels) glow faintly in the dark.
- **Wet ground** turns smooth as it soaks: the sun draws a bright streak on a wet road or a puddle.
- Snow, packed ice and sand get their own roughness from their family.
- **Items, armour and iron golems** (`MATERIALS`): `item.properties` and `entity.properties` number metal and gem items (vanilla tools, armour, ingots, buckets, the house black steel, Create's brass and zinc) and the iron golem like the blocks (10000 + 32 × material), read through `currentRenderedItemId` and `entityId`. Only the texels of the material take it, recognised by their colour (`itemTexel`): the grey blade of an iron sword, not its wooden handle; the cyan of diamond armour; the yellow of gold; the golem's iron, not its vines. They then get the sun's glint and, for metals, the reflected sky and block light, worn, held, dropped or in a frame.

## Resource pack PBR maps

With a resource pack in the labPBR format (`LABPBR`, needs `RELIEF`), Iris binds its `_n` and `_s` maps as `normals` and `specular`. A face whose normal map is neither all black nor a full flat normal (the neutral maps Iris binds without a pack) is taken as having maps, on every block but water and at any distance:

- the normal (rg, z rebuilt) replaces the relief drawn from the texture, and the occlusion (b) multiplies the vertex occlusion;
- the height (a) drives the parallax and the self-shadow instead of the texel brightness, within `RELIEF_RANGE`;
- the specular map replaces the material table: perceptual smoothness (r) gives the roughness, reflectance (g) the F0 below 230 and a metal above, emission (a, below 255) makes the texel glow.

A face with a specular map but no normal map is not detected and keeps the table.

## Reflections by roughness

`ROUGH_REFLECTIONS` (off in Very light) and `ROUGH_REFLECTIONS_STRENGTH`, in `program/deferred1.fsh`. The surface alpha of colortex1 carries the gloss in 1/255 steps, and a half step more marks a metal. For a gloss from 0.15 to 0.6 (the material table, wet ground), one ray per pixel is reflected around a GGX microfacet normal drawn from the roughness (1 - 1.6 × gloss) with a per-frame dither, so the temporal anti-aliasing averages a sharp or blurred reflection. The ray is searched on screen (`WET_REFLECTIONS` steps), then in the voxel volume; the sky is used only when the surface cell is not covered within eight blocks above, otherwise the dim ambient of the place. Dielectrics mix it with a Schlick Fresnel doubled (a polished block shows its surroundings), metals add it tinted by their F0 and keep half of the hemisphere reflection drawn in `solid.fsh`. Puddles and ice (gloss 0.6 and more) keep the mirror path.

## Caves and lava

`CAVE_DETAIL` (off in Very light), `lib/cave.glsl` and `program/mist.fsh`:

- **Molten lava** (family 17, lava and magma): the texel's brightness sets a heat from 0 to 1, mapped through a hot-body ramp (dark red crust, red, orange, pale yellow) and an emission that grows with its square. On the top face, two octaves of slow value noise in texel steps draw plates of cooler crust with sharp edges, a brighter rim around them and ridged veins between them, so a lake no longer repeats the same tile.
- **Heat haze**: in `mist.fsh`, ten steps over the first twenty blocks of the view ray look one and two cells below each point in the voxel volume for lava (light family 2, level 12 or more); the share found shifts the image in waves that rise up the screen. Under `HEAT_SHIMMER`, with the desert haze.
- **Damp rock**: where the sky light is under about 0.25, full and partial ordinary blocks get damp patches (more on ceilings and deeper down) and narrow vertical streaks on walls, darker and with a roughness of 0.16. The texel normal of a damp texel is jittered so block light catches it in points.
- **Block light reflection**: `shade()` exports the block light reaching the point and the direction it comes from (the slope of the voxel light level). Every non-emissive, non-foliage surface adds a GGX reflection of it, with a wider second lobe on damp rock; it shows on metal and wet rock, faintly elsewhere. Needs `COLORED_LIGHT` and `BLOCK_LIGHT_SHAPE`.
- **Dust in the light**: underground (eye sky light low), the motes of `AIR_MOTES` glow in the colour and with the cube of the block light level read in the voxel volume.
- **Glowing blocks** keep a more saturated colour (glow lichen, froglights).

## Block relief

Without a normal-map resource pack, `lib/relief.glsl` draws a relief from the textures themselves, on full blocks, sand and partial blocks (not on plants, leaves, water, ice, glass or glowing blocks), within `RELIEF_RANGE` blocks (24 by default; it fades over the last 40 %).

- **Height of a texel**: its luminance against the mean of its texture (mip level 4 of the atlas, one pixel for a 16-pixel texture). A darker texel is a hollow, a lighter one a bump. Everything is read at texel centres, so the relief stays on the 1/16 grid, in steps, never smooth. The texture rectangle comes from `mc_midTexCoord` (its centre) and the distance of each corner to it (its half-size), so a relief that crosses an edge wraps to the opposite edge, as the texture repeats.
- **Texel normals** (`RELIEF_STRENGTH`): the slope toward the four neighbours tilts the normal, in the face frame (`at_tangent`). Direct light follows the tilt, measured against the face's own lighting, so the shadow map and its bias are unchanged; the sky light drops a little in texels that turn away from the open air.
- **Parallax occlusion** (`RELIEF_STEPS`: off, 8, 12, 20; off in Very light and Light, 12 in Balanced, 20 in Beautiful; `RELIEF_DEPTH`, 0.06 block by default): the eye ray marches down through the height layers until it meets the relief. At a grazing angle, bumps hide the hollows behind them and mortar lines shift. Fewer steps are taken when looking straight at the face.
- **Relief shadows** (`RELIEF_SHADOWS`): from the point seen, six steps climb toward the sun or moon; a bump above the ray shades the hollow, more softly the farther it is.
- **Block edges** (`RELIEF_BEVEL`): the outer texel of each face leans outward, so the edges of blocks catch the light.

## Anti-aliasing and profiles

- **SMAA** (`SMAA`: off, medium, high, ultra; on the first page of the settings) smooths the stair-stepped silhouettes of blocks, plants and creatures. Edges are found on the luminance of the finished image, then kept only where the depth jumps by more than 2 % between neighbours: the pixels of the textures stay sharp, only shapes are smoothed. The grade moved from `final` to `composite2` so that SMAA works on the image as it will be shown.
- **Temporal anti-aliasing** (`TAA`, off in the Very light profile): the camera is shifted by a fraction of a pixel each frame (eight positions), and `composite3` blends the new frame with the history, reprojected from the depth and the previous camera. The history is clipped to the box of the current neighbours (mean and spread in YCoCg) and read with a Catmull-Rom filter, so a moving shape leaves no trail and the history does not soften frame after frame. Pixels of texture larger than a screen pixel keep mostly the current frame and stay sharp; the full blend only applies where the texture's pixels become finer than the screen, in the distance, where they shimmered. `final` then gives back a little of the texture grain, clamped to the neighbouring colours so no light rim appears. The hand is not reprojected, the sky only follows the camera's rotation.
- **Finer temporal anti-aliasing**: the colour box that holds the history narrows when the point moves on screen (from 1.25 to 0.85 standard deviations), the blend is weighted by brightness so a flickering highlight (water glint, snow sparkle) no longer dominates, and the history keeps in its alpha the distance of each point (`TAA_DISOCCLUSION`): when the distance found there does not match where the point stood, something has just uncovered it, and its history is mostly dropped, so no ghost trails around the hand, mobs or trunks.
- **Sharpening** (`TAA_SHARPEN`, 0.45 by default): the texture grain softened by the temporal anti-aliasing is given back in `final`, contrast-adaptively: stronger in soft areas, held back where the neighbours already differ a lot, and never past the neighbouring colours.
- **Motion blur** (`MOTION_BLUR`: off, light, medium, strong, very strong; medium by default, off in the Very light profile): `composite4` takes twelve samples along the path each point took on screen since the previous frame (`lib/reproject.glsl`, shared with the temporal anti-aliasing), over a quarter of a frame per step of the setting, so the streak follows the frame rate like a film camera's shutter. It runs after the temporal anti-aliasing so the history stays sharp. The streak is capped at 6 % of the screen height, tiny motions (walking, head bob) barely blur, the hand never blurs and never bleeds into the landscape. Only the camera's motion counts: an animal running across a still view stays sharp.
- **Four profiles**: Très léger (very light: no reflections, no screen occlusion, no shafts, the game's clouds, 1024 shadow map over 64 blocks with hard edges, no anti-aliasing), Léger, Équilibré (the default) and Beau. Every profile sets every option it touches, so switching back and forth restores them.

## Cost

- **Passes switched off**: `program.<pass>.enabled` takes an option name; Iris replaces it by the option's value, 0 disables the pass and any other number keeps it (with a "unknown option" warning in the log for values other than 0 and 1, checked on the bench with `TAA`). The clouds and near mist (`composite`, `CLOUD_STEPS || FOG_STEPS`), occlusion and bounce (`deferred`, `AO_SAMPLES || SSGI`) and the first two SMAA passes (`composite5`, `composite6`, `SMAA`) stop when their setting is 0. Their buffer formats and clear colours are declared in passes that always run (`deferred1`, `mist`), and the clear colours mean "no clouds", "no fog" and "no occlusion".
- **Early depth test on terrain**: the far-shadow height map is written per vertex in `gbuffers_terrain.vsh` and `dh_terrain.vsh`, not per pixel, so the terrain fragment shaders no longer write to an image and keep the early depth test.
- **Near mist at half resolution**: `fogMarch` (`lib/fogmarch.glsl`) runs in the half-resolution `composite` pass into `colortex11` (light, transmittance); `mist.fsh` brings it back with a four-tap bilinear filter weighted by depth.
- **Occlusion upsampling**: `deferred1` reads the half-resolution occlusion and bounce from the four nearest cells, weighted bilinearly, by depth and by normal, instead of a 4 × 4 blur; the temporal anti-aliasing smooths the rest.
- **Caps in the Beautiful profile**: 20 wave trains, 20 water reflection steps, 64 voxel reflection steps, 16 cloud steps and 6 cloud light steps (the higher values stay available). Whitecap trails follow only the larger half of the wave trains, skipping those finer than a texel.
- The game's dryness half-life is 1 200 ticks both in `shaders.properties` and in the shader's constant.

## The mist

- A layer whose density halves about every 11 blocks above the ground around the player (`MIST_HEIGHT`). Barely there at noon (near air stays clear), more at night, thickest at dawn and dusk and in the rain; more in a humid biome, almost none near the player in a desert.
- **Marched in volume near the player** (`FOG_STEPS`: 0, 8, 12, 16, 24 steps; 0 in the Very light profile, 8 in Light, 12 in Balanced, 16 in Beautiful) over `FOG_RANGE` blocks (48 to 128, 96 by default). The steps grow with the square of the distance (fine near the eye, coarse far away) and are shifted by a per-pixel dither that the temporal anti-aliasing averages. Each step is integrated exactly (Beer-Lambert over its length), so the result does not depend on the number of steps, only its detail does. With `FOG_STEPS` at 0 the former analytic veil comes back unchanged.
- **Banks** (`FOG_BILLOWS`, 0 to 1.5): two octaves of the cloud noise, one wide (about 55 blocks) and one fine (about 16), shape the density into banks and holes. They drift with the wind of the `fallow` mod (its direction and strength), the fine octave faster than the wide one, and slowly rise.
- **Sun and moon through it**: each step reads the shadow map (and the tint of stained glass) inside its range, so the mist is lit only where the light reaches: rays under the trees, shadow behind a wall, coloured beams behind a window. Forward scattering of the mist (two lobes, one forward and one backward) and of clear air (a sharper forward lobe) is computed per step: the air alone, even without mist, draws the rays where foliage cuts the sun. Both need `LIGHT_SHAFTS`.
- **Block light in the mist** (`FOG_BLOCK_GLOW`, 0 to 2; needs `COLORED_LIGHT`): each step reads the coloured light volume, so a torch, a lantern or a lava pool wraps itself in a halo of its colour, stronger where the mist is dense. The light falls with the cube of its level, so a halo stays a few blocks wide.
- **Cave haze** (`CAVE_HAZE`, 0 to 1.5): under the ground (the sky light the eye receives is low) a faint dust replaces the mist. It is invisible in the dark and only shows around torches and in the beams that fall through an opening.
- Each open clearing (the four nearest) removes the mist inside a vertical cylinder of its radius, with a soft edge; the opening wave is followed. Standing in a clearing lifts part of the mist of the whole view (`MIST_LIFT`, 70 %).
- **Beyond the marched range**, the rest of the layer is still integrated exactly, and a ring closes the horizon at the render distance, or at the Distant Horizons distance capped by `MIST_RING_MAX` (512 blocks). It does not lift. This far part takes the colour of the low sky in that direction: at sunset it glows on the side of the sun. It is applied first, then the marched near part in front of it.

## What the `fallow` mod gives the shader (`rendu` module)

A mixin on Iris adds uniforms any program may declare. Without the mod they read zero and the shader falls back.

| Uniform | Content | Fallback |
|---|---|---|
| `brumaireLink` | 1 when the module runs | 0 |
| `brumaireMist` | x: share of mist lifted around the camera; y: smoothed height of the ground | no lift; a little under the camera |
| `brumaireClearing0..3` | Centre seen from the camera, radius, strength of the four nearest clearings | no hole |
| `brumaireWind` | Direction, strength and turbulence of the pack's wind (`vent` module) | a steady wind, stronger in the rain |
| `brumairePush0..3` | Feet and reach of the living beings nearest the camera | no grass pushed aside |
| `brumaireWake0..5`, `brumaireWakeSpeed0..1` | Surface point, half width and speed of the boats and swimmers nearest the camera | no wakes |
| `brumaireGust0..1` | The two gust fronts nearest to passing | no gusts |

## Other mods

- **Distant Horizons**: `dh_terrain` and `dh_water` light the far landscape like the near one; it gives way to real chunks near the camera.
- **Create (Flywheel)**: Colorwheel refuses a shader pack without `clrwl_gbuffers`; ours lights Flywheel models like entities and gives them a shadow.
- **Sable and Aeronautics (Veil)**: Iris Veil Compat builds its programs from ours; levitite compiles against the block, entity and shadow programs.
- **LambDynamicLights** provides held lights; the shader adds none.

## Verified on the bench (full pack, software rendering)

The pack loads without error, day, sunset, night and rain render, a clearing opened with `/fallow clairiere 32` opens the mist, a Create water wheel, a cow, glowstone and a lantern are lit at night. Frame rate was not measured: the bench has no graphics card.
